# Exact Laurent–Taylor candidate bound

Research input only. The finite arithmetic is regenerated by `laurent_taylor_bound.py`; the physical interpretation, coefficient replay, and analytic exponential-remainder bridge still require Lean checking.

The pinned `physical_algebra.py::corner_algebra` regenerates the physical invariant as a finite Laurent polynomial `R(a,b,c,d)=sum_e c_e a^e0 b^e1 c^e2 d^e3`, with 2,083 integer coefficients. Its star invariance is checked. At the 2442 seed, substitute

`(a,b,c,d)=(omega exp(iu0), omega^2 exp(iu1), omega^2 exp(iu2), omega exp(iu3))`.

Each term becomes `c_e omega^(e0+2e1+2e2+e3) exp(i(e dot u))`. Expand that last exponential through degree 16. The coefficient of `u^alpha` is the real part of

`sum_e c_e omega^(e0+2e1+2e2+e3) i^|alpha| product_j e_j^alpha_j / product_j alpha_j!`.

These coefficients are exact rationals for even degree and exact rationals times `sqrt(3)` for odd degree. The code also checks that every imaginary coefficient vanishes. Degrees 0 through 3 vanish; degree 4 is exactly `243(u0 u3-u1 u2)^2`, whose coefficient absolute sum is 972. All 4,538 nonzero coefficients through degree 16 are retained in `LAURENT_TAYLOR_COEFFICIENTS.json`. The homogeneous coefficient absolute sums are recorded in `LAURENT_TAYLOR_BOUND.json`, with odd-degree `sqrt(3)` factors shown explicitly.

For `rho=max|u_j|` and degree `d`, the absolute value of that homogeneous polynomial is at most its coefficient absolute sum times `rho^d`. Bound `sqrt(3)<26/15` for odd degrees. No division by the quartic determinant cone is used.

For each finite frequency, the real-axis integral remainder gives

`|exp(i(e dot u)) - sum_(m=0)^16 (i(e dot u))^m/m!| <= |e dot u|^17/17! <= ||e||_1^17 rho^17/17!`.

Triangle inequality over the finite Laurent terms bounds the full tail by

`T rho^17`, where `T = sum_e |c_e| ||e||_1^17 / 17!`.

The exact `T` is retained in the JSON result. The maximum frequency norm is 16.
