# Rank and spectral necessary conditions on the common-ray quotient

## Header

**Statement.** Let H satisfy the hypotheses of
[FiniteCommonRayAlgebra.md](FiniteCommonRayAlgebra.md), and let
T_1,…,T_5 be the commuting invertible coordinate multiplication matrices of the
residual algebra B_H, of dimension r=180−2m(H). Build Γ_j from the
commutative determinant det_r(zI_r+L) (Section 3) and
B_w=S·Srev restricted to the exterior square (Section 6). If (I,H/√6)
lies in k+2 MUBs, then:

- nullity Γ_3 ≥ 6k. More strongly, the left kernel of [Γ_0 … Γ_4] has
  dimension at least 6k. A quartet (k=2) forces
  rank[Γ_0 … Γ_4] ≤ r−12 = 168−2m(H).
- If rank Γ_3 = r−6 and a third basis exists, that basis is unique.
- z^(15k)(z−6)^(18k(k−1)) divides det(zI−B_w). For a quartet this is
  z^30(z−6)^36, so Q6(H)=det(6I−B_w)=0.

**Status.** Analytic proof. These are necessary-condition tools: the
statements do not exclude any H. There is no verifier. The source's
certificates, which apply them on the two-circulant surface, are not
imported (see Provenance).

**Dependencies.** Only the representation property (1.1) below. For every H
with all 3-by-3 minors nonzero it is supplied by
[FiniteCommonRayAlgebra.md](FiniteCommonRayAlgebra.md), Sections 3–6 (the
Gorenstein quotient B_H, which contains every physical local algebra). There
are no unproved inputs.

**Provenance.** Archive `03_algebraic/global_locus/supplied/PROOF.md`,
Sections 2, 3, 4 and 6 (copied). Section 1 of the source constructed the
180-dimensional module only on the totally nonsingular locus from
archive-only packages; here it is replaced by B_H, with r=180−2m(H). Omitted:
Section 4's "global polynomial versions" (they need the archive's
division-free X_i, ell, w construction), Section 5 (generic third-basis
uniqueness on the two-circulant surface Sigma), Section 7 (its modular
certificates, with C++ checker), and Section 8 (saturation).

## 1. Input: the residual common-vector representation

Use unnormalized Hadamards, with unit-modulus entries and HH*=6I. A vector
unbiased to the standard basis is x/sqrt(6), where x=(1,x1,...,x5) and |xi|=1.
It is also unbiased to H/sqrt(6) exactly when

    (sum_r x_r/H_rj)(sum_s H_sj/x_s)=6,  j=0,...,5.

[FiniteCommonRayAlgebra.md](FiniteCommonRayAlgebra.md) proves that, for every
H with nonzero 3-by-3 minors, this algebra is finite of length 252−2m(H). The
72 universal nonphysical solutions are removed by a canonical quotient B_H of
dimension r=180−2m(H), with commuting invertible coordinate matrices
T1,...,T5.

The ONLY property needed for the necessity theorems is:

    every physical common vector x gives a nonzero row v_x,
    v_x T_i = x_i v_x.                                      (1.1)

This holds for B_H: evaluation at a physical x factors through B_H
(FiniteCommonRayAlgebra.md, Section 6), and v_x is that character written
as a row vector. It never vanishes, since it takes the value 1 on the unit.
The result is valid even at nonreduced common-vector fibres. A
change of evaluation pivots conjugates all coordinate matrices and does not
change the rank statements.

The old pair operator is

    S = I_(r^2) + sum_i T_i^(-1) tensor T_i.                  (1.2)

We retain the distinction between total nonsingularity, product regularity,
and simplicity of common-vector roots. They are different conditions. The
necessity results below do NOT require distinct product roots or simple
common-vector roots. They require the stated finite representation.

## 2. Distinct physical vectors give independent evaluation rows

For any finite collection x^(1),...,x^(m) of distinct normalized phase vectors,
choose scalars c_i so that the numbers sum_i c_i x_i^(a) are all distinct. Such
choices exist: only finitely many proper hyperplanes in coefficient space
are forbidden. Then v_(x^(a)) are left eigenvectors of sum_i c_i T_i with
pairwise distinct eigenvalues. Therefore they are linearly independent.

This proof uses the evaluation relation (1.1), not numerical diagonalization.
It works in the presence of unrelated repeated roots or nilpotent blocks.

## 3. The explicit r-by-r matrix Gamma_3

Let C be the commutative matrix algebra generated by the T_i and their inverses.
For a,b=1,...,r, form the r-by-r matrix with entries in C

    L_ab = delta_ab I_r + sum_i (T_i)_ab T_i^(-1).

The usual determinant is well-defined because its entries commute. Define

    det_r(z I_r + L) = sum_(j=0)^r Gamma_j z^j,              (3.1)

where each Gamma_j is itself an r-by-r ordinary matrix in C. Our matrix is

    Gamma_3(H) = coefficient of z^3 in (3.1).

Thus Gamma_3 is a finite expression in the coordinate matrices and hence a
rational function of H on the chosen finite chart. It has NO additional
unknown basis vectors. No numerical eigenvalues occur in its definition.

For a physical common vector x, apply the character in (1.1) to the commuting
entries of L. The scalar matrix obtained is

    L(x) = I_r + sum_i x_i^(-1) T_i.

It follows that

    v_x Gamma_j = gamma_j(x) v_x,
    gamma_j(x) = [z^j] det(z I_r + L(x)).                    (3.2)

### An efficient univariate form

On a cyclic chart, the coordinate algebra is represented as K[t]/q(t), and
T_i=x_i(T), T_i^(-1)=y_i(T), with q monic of degree r. Set

    s(t,u)=1+sum_i y_i(t)x_i(u).

Then Gamma_j is multiplication by

    gamma_j(t) = [z^j] Res_u(q(u), z+s(t,u)) mod q(t).        (3.3)

This is a computational formula for the same intrinsic matrix. The abstract
commutative determinant (3.1), rather than a guessed separating coordinate,
defines the matrix at other fibers.

## 4. Global basis-rank theorem

THEOREM. Suppose (I,H/sqrt(6)) is contained in a set of k+2 mutually unbiased
bases. Then

    nullity Gamma_3(H) >= 6k.                              (4.1)

Consequently,

    number of MUBs containing the pair
       <= 2 + floor(nullity Gamma_3(H)/6).                 (4.2)

PROOF. Pick a vector x from any one of the k additional bases. Its other five
basis vectors y give independent left null rows v_y of L(x), since

    v_y L(x) = (1+sum_i y_i/x_i)v_y = 0.

Thus det(zI+L(x)) is divisible by z^5. In particular gamma_3(x)=0, and (3.2)
puts v_x in the left kernel of Gamma_3. Across the k bases there are 6k distinct
rays, because vectors in different mutually unbiased bases cannot coincide.
Section 2 makes their evaluation rows independent. This proves (4.1). QED.

The same proof actually gives v_x Gamma_j=0 for j=0,1,2,3,4. One may use the
concatenated matrix [Gamma_0 ... Gamma_4] for a still stronger test.

COROLLARY. Every third-basis point satisfies all (r-5)-by-(r-5) minors of
Gamma_3 equal to zero. Every quartet-compatible point satisfies all
(r-11)-by-(r-11) minors equal to zero. (For r=180 these are the 175- and
169-minors.) These are restrictions on EVERY component, not a family
assumption.

COROLLARY (uniqueness). If rank Gamma_3=r-6 and a third basis exists, that
basis is unique as an unordered set of rays.

Indeed its six evaluation rows span the six-dimensional kernel. Any different
third basis would contain a seventh ray in that kernel, contradicting Section 2.
This argument rules out different completion branches, including ones far from
any explicitly constructed completion.

## 6. A second global invariant: the missing cross-basis condition

(Numbering follows the source; its Section 5, on the two-circulant surface,
is not imported.)

Set T_0=I_r and define

    S = sum_(i=0)^5 T_i^(-1) tensor T_i,
    Srev = sum_(i=0)^5 T_i tensor T_i^(-1),
    B = S*Srev.

S and Srev commute. B commutes with the tensor flip and restricts to an
operator B_w on the exterior square, of dimension r(r-1)/2 (16110 when
r=180).

For distinct physical common vectors x,y,

    (v_x wedge v_y) B_w = b(x,y) (v_x wedge v_y),
    b(x,y)=(sum_i y_i/x_i)(sum_i x_i/y_i)=|sum_i y_i/x_i|^2.

Thus b=0 for orthogonal normalized vectors and b=6 for mutually unbiased
normalized vectors. The latter relation is not present in the old pair
obstruction.

THEOREM. If k extra MUBs extend the fixed pair, then

    z^[15k] (z-6)^[18k(k-1)] divides det(z I-B_w).            (6.1)

PROOF. The 6k distinct evaluation rows are independent. Their exterior
products are therefore independent. The k bases supply 15k unordered
orthogonal pairs, each with eigenvalue 0. Between different bases there are
36*C(k,2)=18k(k-1) unordered cross-pairs, each with eigenvalue 6. These give the
stated geometric eigenspaces and hence the characteristic factors. QED.

In particular, a quartet forces zero eigenspace dimension at least 30 and
six-eigenspace dimension at least 36. If

    Q6(H)=det(6I-B_w),

then a quartet forces Q6=0. More strongly, in any smooth regular parameter
chart every partial derivative of Q6 of total order below 36 vanishes at a
quartet point. This follows from corank at least 36: constant row/column
operations put the matrix at that point in a form with at least 36 zero rows,
so every determinant term has Taylor order at least 36. No codimension count
is inferred merely from this multiplicity.

### Exact determinant reductions

Simultaneously triangularize the commuting coordinate matrices. Their diagonal
characters, with multiplicities, give s_ab for S and b_ab=s_ab*s_ba for B. Even
when the matrices have nilpotent parts, this proves

    det S = 6^r det B_w,
    det(B-6I) = 30^r [det(B_w-6I)]^2.                       (6.2)

The first identity identifies the old pair determinant. The second permits
nonvanishing of Q6 to be checked by a resultant in the ordered pair algebra.
No square-root choice is needed for nonvanishing.

Both invariants are preserved by the legitimate Hadamard equivalences. A
change of coordinates sending normalized x to x' rescales s(x,y) by a nonzero
ratio, but the reciprocal factor s(y,x) cancels it. Hence b(x,y), B_w and its
characteristic polynomial are intrinsically defined.

## 9. What has not been proved

It is not shown that physical points with rank [Γ_0 … Γ_4] ≤ r−12, or with
Q6=0, are absent, or that they force a 2-by-2 Hadamard block. These are
necessary conditions only. Their strata have been neither computed nor
excluded for any family of H relevant to a quartet.
