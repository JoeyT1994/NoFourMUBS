# Bounded general-Hadamard minimax pilot — numerical evidence only

This is an exploratory calculation, not a proof or a covering certificate. It was requested as a bounded check of the critical-point strategy on general order-six Hadamards, beyond the Fourier families.

## Method

`explore_minimax.py` starts from reproducibly seeded random dephased phase matrices and first solves the full Hadamard orthogonality equations by least squares with their analytic Jacobian. It then minimizes the maximum absolute signed corner invariant, which equals `sqrt(M)` at Hadamard matrices.

At each feasible iterate it:

1. Evaluates all signed corner invariants and their analytic derivatives using the 48-term Laurent expansion from `verify_tube.py`.
2. Computes the numerical nullspace of the Hadamard Jacobian.
3. Solves an epigraph linear program for the maximum of **all** positive and negative signed linearizations in that tangent space, within a step bound.
4. Retracts the proposed step using Newton least-squares correction to the Hadamard equations.
5. Accepts only a step that actually decreases the numerical objective and passes the residual and local-retraction checks.

This is constrained minimax descent. No unconstrained softmax objective is substituted for `M`.

The numerical rank threshold is `2e-7`. Therefore a reported first-order stationary point is a numerical candidate under that threshold, not a certified critical point. A zero-dimensional numerical tangent likewise does not establish algebraic isolation.

## Recorded 30-start run

The pseudorandom seed is `2026100801`. Thirty starts completed with a requested cap of 120 seconds; the recorded run, including Fourier-orbit alignment, took approximately 4.38 seconds in the supplied environment.

| Final numerical outcome | Starts | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| `M` between approximately `6e-25` and `9.05e-12` | 12 | Reached an almost Fourier matrix |
| `M ≈ 205.421318589` | 7 | Repeated first-order minimax stationary candidates |
| `M ≈ 250.739727562` | 1 | First-order minimax stationary candidate |
| `M ≈ 256` | 3 | First-order minimax stationary candidates |
| `M ≈ 243`, numerical tangent dimension zero | 7 | Isolated-looking candidates; isolation/equivalence not certified |

The seven repeated `205.421318589` candidates have the same numerically rounded full corner spectrum. Each has 24 active signed-corner constraints whose tangent gradients span four dimensions. This is suggestive of a repeated stationary equivalence class, but no exact classification is claimed.

Every final matrix with `M<60` was checked against all 7,200 dephased Fourier-orbit representatives generated by the supplied orbit routine. The largest resulting **floating-point upper bound** on `nu` was approximately `1.74e-7`. Thus this run found no low-`M` candidate suggesting a distant component.

The largest final Hadamard residual was approximately `2.26e-12`. The largest discrepancy between the signed Laurent formula squared and the supplied corner evaluator at the starting Hadamards was approximately `1.82e-12`. A central-difference directional check of the analytic signed-invariant gradient had absolute discrepancy approximately `1.22e-8`.

These checks support the numerical implementation. They do not turn any run into a proof.

## Reproduction and data

All initial random phases, projected Hadamard phases, final phases, iteration traces, stopping reasons, residuals, and low-`M` Fourier alignment bounds are saved in `minimax_results.json`. The concise standard output is in `run_log.txt`.

Run the script within the delivered output archive. It locates the bundled `../inputs/toolkit/common.py` and `../global/verify_tube.py` relative to its own path:

```sh
python3 explore_minimax.py --starts 30 --seconds 120 --seed 2026100801
```

It requires NumPy and SciPy. The recorded environment used one BLAS thread and the supplied dependency directory.

## What remains open

Thirty random starts do not exhaust any parameter domain. Newton projection and descent can miss components or basins. No interval enclosure, finite domain coverage, stationary-point classification, or global exclusion follows from this pilot. In particular it does not prove that every local minimum below 26, below 60, or below 144 is Fourier-equivalent.

## Supplemental near-active partition observations

`explore_active_patterns.py` replays the saved trajectories and records 98 accepted points with `0.01<M<60` from 12 trajectories. It groups a corner with its forced equal diagonal-complement corner. A full partition means an unordered row 3+3 split together with an unordered column 3+3 split.

With the numerical near-active tolerance `max(1e-7,1e-8*M)`, 77 points had one near-active corner class, 20 had two classes on different partitions, and one had three classes on three different partitions. In the two-class cases, seven pairs shared a row partition and thirteen shared a column partition. The three-class case formed three edges of a two-by-two grid of row and column partitions. None of these observations consisted of the two complementary classes within one partition.

**These were near-ties, not established exact ties:** the gaps were approximately `1e-8` to `4e-7`. At a looser one-percent tolerance, many points near Fourier had 36 near-active corner classes across 18 partitions. The observations therefore suggest shared-row and shared-column cross-partition cases as useful targets, but establish no restriction on an arbitrary exact active set.

All recorded phases, class labels, top invariant values, and thresholds are in `active_patterns.json`. Run `python3 explore_active_patterns.py` to reproduce this bounded follow-up after the initial minimax run.
