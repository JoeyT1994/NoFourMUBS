\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $H$ be an order-six complex Hadamard, and suppose its dephased
leading corner is
\[
 E=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\1&e^{i\theta_1}&e^{i\theta_2}
                 \\1&e^{i\theta_3}&e^{i\theta_4}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Assume $0<R(E)=M(H)<5$ and
\[
 \left|\theta_j-\theta_j^*\right|\le\frac1{1024},\qquad
 \theta^*=\frac\pi3(1,2,2,4).
\]
Then $H$ has an equivalent representative $F_6\circ e^{iu}$ with
$u$ horizontal and $\|u\|_F<6/25<3/10$. Consequently
$d_F(H)<1/30$ by the established local theorem.
\end{theorem}

The angle differences are circular; the stated representatives are
used in the certificate. The criterion remains valid after any corner
framing whose transformed seed cube is contained in this cube.
Transposition is legitimate for the horizontal Frobenius norm
conclusion: it transposes $u$ and preserves its norm.

\section*{1. Keep the two reconstruction sheets together}

The horizontal side uses $z$ and the transposed vertical side uses

The fixed-corner exhaustion theorem permits exactly these matched

\section*{2. Bound the horizontal phase directly}
Fix the explicit Fourier reference $F$ with row and column orders
\[
 (0,1,2,5,3,4).
\]
For every entry, the checker proves
$\Re(\mathcal H_{jk}/F_{jk})>0$ on each rectangle. Define
\[
 V_{jk}(\theta,h)=\Ima\log(\mathcal H_{jk}(\theta,h)/F_{jk}),
 \qquad U=PV,
\]
where $P$ subtracts row means and column means and adds the overall
mean. On an actual completion the entries have modulus one, so
$\mathcal H=F\circ e^{iV}$. Removing the row and column phases gives
the equivalent representative $F\circ e^{iU}$, with $U$ horizontal.

For a rectangle centred at $x_0$ with half-widths $r_j$, the
fundamental theorem of calculus on its line segments gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bound}
 \|U(x)\|_F\le \|U(x_0)\|_F+
      \sum_{j=1}^{5}r_j\sup_{y\text{ in rectangle}}
                         \|\partial_j U(y)\|_F.
\end{equation}
All quantities on the right are bounded outwards. This remains valid
when the centre itself is not a physical reconstruction point.

The derivatives are computed by the product and chain rules using
complex interval arithmetic. For example, if $w=f(v)$,
\[
 w_j=f'(v)v_j,\qquad
 w_{jk}=f'(v)v_{jk}+f''(v)v_jv_k.
\]
Conjugation, real and imaginary parts act componentwise because the
five variables are real. Every intermediate value enclosure is
intersected with its centred mean-value enclosure. Likewise, its
gradient enclosure is intersected with the enclosure obtained from
its centre gradient and interval Hessian:
\begin{align*}
 f(B)&\subseteq f(x_0)+\sum_j(B_j-x_{0j})\partial_jf(B),\\
 \partial_jf(B)&\subseteq\partial_jf(x_0)
              +\sum_k(B_k-x_{0k})\partial_{jk}f(B).
\end{align*}
These are rigorous intersections of independently valid enclosures.
The Hessian is used to tighten derivatives; the accepted norm bound
is always \eqref{eq:bound}. Every norm used for acceptance must be
finite. A failed domain, branch, divisor or finiteness test rejects
the attempted certificate.

\section*{4. Another useful entrance criterion}
\begin{lemma}
Suppose $H$ and an explicit Fourier reference $F$ are raw phase
matrices, and each normalized column has Euclidean error at most
$1/21$. Then, after row and column phasing, the horizontal phase
distance satisfies
$\|u\|_F^2\le24/293<9/100$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write the column bound as $L$. Each raw entry error is at most
$\sqrt6L$, and the raw Frobenius error is at most $6L$. For a unit
phase of chord error $t$, the principal angular error is
$2\arcsin(t/2)\le t/\sqrt{1-6L^2/4}$, by bounding the derivative
of $2\arcsin(t/2)$ on the permitted interval. Hence the phase
Frobenius norm squared is at most
\[
 \frac{36L^2}{1-3L^2/2}\le\frac{24}{293}<\frac9{100}.
\]
The horizontal projection is an orthogonal projection and cannot
increase the norm. Its removed row and column terms are exactly
permitted equivalence phases.
\end{proof}

This lemma allows existing complete side-cover estimates to enter
the local theorem as well. The new active-cube certificate instead
uses the reconstruction directly and retains its matched-side
correlation.
