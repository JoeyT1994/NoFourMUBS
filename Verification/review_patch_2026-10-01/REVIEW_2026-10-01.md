## 1. FastTM rescaled intrinsics

### 1.1 The normalization is an enclosure, not an exact interval equality

Write an actual represented function as

\[
a(s)=c_0+h(s),\qquad s\in[-1,1]^4.
\]

The model obtained by copying the coefficients and replacing its constant coefficient by zero encloses this particular `h`, with the original remainder retained. Let

\[
L=1/\operatorname{interval}(c_0),\qquad
v=1+Lh.
\]

The interval-times-model primitive encloses multiplication by **every** scalar in `L`, hence by the true `1/c0`. Consequently `v` contains `1+h(s)/c0` pointwise. Its stored constant coefficient is exactly 1: multiplication of the zero constant by a finite midpoint gives zero, and the subsequent addition of binary64 1 is exact. Rounding and interval-width allowances remain in the remainder; they do not alter that coefficient.

Thus the recomputed

\[
x=\operatorname{hbound}(v)
\]

bounds the actual `|h(s)/c0|`, including normalization overestimation. Both routines explicitly test this **new** bound against 1 (`fasttm.jl:282–294`). The initial test involving `hbound(a)/|c0|` is not silently substituted for it. This second check is important: the first inequality alone need not imply that the computed normalized enclosure passes.

Using `L` again outside the series does not create an invalid correlation assumption. Both interval multiplications overenclose all permitted scalar choices; the true scalar is one such choice at each occurrence. Loss of correlation can widen the answer, not remove the true answer.

### 1.2 Reciprocal tail and prefactor

For `|u|≤x<1`,

\[
\frac1{1+u}-\sum_{k=0}^{Q}(-u)^k
=\frac{(-u)^{Q+1}}{1+u},\qquad
\left|\frac{(-u)^{Q+1}}{1+u}\right|
\le\frac{x^{Q+1}}{1-x}.
\]

`series(v,...)` evaluates the finite polynomial using the center-free part of `v` and adds the outward tail bound. Multiplication of the **entire resulting model**, including its remainder, by `L` supplies the factor `1/c0`. There is no omitted `1/|c0|` in the final error allowance. Negative nonzero centers are allowed for reciprocal; the argument does not rely on a positive center.

### 1.3 Square-root tail, including Q=0

With `c0>0`, the same normalization ensures the actual argument lies on the positive real branch. The coefficients satisfy

\[
b_0=1,\qquad b_{k+1}=b_k\frac{1/2-k}{k+1}.
\]

For every `k≥1`, their absolute-value ratio is below 1. The tail starts at `Q+1≥1`, so

\[
\left|\sum_{k=Q+1}^{\infty}b_ku^k\right|
\le |b_{Q+1}|\frac{x^{Q+1}}{1-x}
\]

also holds at `Q=0`. The interval recurrence encloses the true coefficient, including its sign. The implementation multiplies the resulting model by `sqrt(X)` for `X=interval(c0)`; this is an outward interval square root.

**Suggested notation clarification, not an implementation error:** the note's `interval(√c0)` should be written `sqrt(interval(c0))`. Constructing a singleton interval from an already rounded scalar square root would not generally enclose the exact square root. Production uses the correct order.

### 1.4 Guarded-primitives claim

The new model-valued arithmetic uses the previously guarded interval multiplication, scalar addition, Horner products, constants, and remainder padding. Clearing a copied constant coefficient is an exact polynomial edit already allowed by the invariant; it is not a floating subtraction with a discarded error. The interval backend remains part of the trust base.

The binomial recurrence uses exactly representable small integer/half-integer operands. The final interval prefactors pass through the interval operand guard. An out-of-range reciprocal prefactor, tail, or output model is not licensed merely because the analytic series converges: the arithmetic guard can still reject it. The improvement does not establish that every small-center input succeeds.

The claim is appropriately about **accepted guarded traces**, not unrestricted external coefficient mutation, an independently verified interval library, or all historical campaign runs.

## 2. Symmetric signed-corner prune

**Verdict: correct, conditional on the stated accepted inputs.**

Reviewed locations: `ProvenResults/SymmetricSignedCornerPrune/PROOF.md:8–170`, `predicate.py`, `interval_core.py`, and `verify.py`.

### 2.1 Polarized moments and cancellation

For the actual three missing columns, write `M_pq=Σ x_l^p y_l^q` and `Pi=st−M_11`. The six moments used in the proof are

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_{10}&=s,&M_{01}&=t,&M_{1,-1}&=\bar r,\\
M_{11}&=st-\Pi,&
M_{2,-1}&=s\bar r-u\bar\Pi,&
M_{-1,2}&=tr-v\bar\Pi.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last two follow by expanding the cross terms, using the products `u=Πx_l`, `v=Πy_l` and reciprocal conjugation of unit phases. The verifier checks the corresponding Laurent identities in independent nonzero variables, rather than at numerical samples.

The genuinely additional premise is that the accepted side formulas give the physical products and mixed moment:

\[
\Pi=z\lambda_0,\quad u=\lambda_0\bar\lambda_1,
\quad v=\lambda_0\bar\lambda_2,\quad |\lambda_j|=1.
\]

For example, unitarity of `lambda0` gives

\[
u\bar\Pi=\bar z\bar\lambda_1
=\frac{g\bar z+k_1z\bar z}{P_1}
=\frac{\chi+2k_1\eta-i\sigma\sqrt R}{2P_1}.
\]

Likewise,

\[
\Pi=\frac{\chi+2k_0\eta+i\sigma\sqrt R}{2\bar P_0},
\qquad
v\bar\Pi=\frac{\chi+2k_2\eta-i\sigma\sqrt R}{2P_2}.
\]

Here `z zbar=eta` follows from `R=4g²eta−chi²`. These cancellations genuinely remove `1/g` from the evaluated expressions. Symmetric moments remove the cube-root choice and column ordering. No individual side root, `1/sqrt(R)`, or uncancelled `1/g` remains. The `P_j` denominators do remain and must be separated from zero on the **whole box**.

### 2.2 Sign, normalization, and mean-square argument

The verifier independently reconstructs the 6-by-6 determinant and checks the actual six implemented Laurent coefficients, their conjugate partners, and transpose invariance. In this module `f=iD`; since `D` is imaginary, `f` is real and `f²=−D²`. A different overall sign convention elsewhere does not change a square, but the internal alpha/beta signs must be consistent; they are.

With `h1=d_11/bar(P0)`, `h2=d_(2,-1)/P1`, and `h3=d_(-1,2)/P2`, the signed mean is

\[
\frac13\sum_{l=0}^2 f(T_{jkl})
=-\frac23\operatorname{Im}Z_0+
\frac{\sigma\sqrt R}{3}\operatorname{Re}(h_1-h_2-h_3)
=\alpha_{jk}+\sigma\beta_{jk}\sqrt R.
\]

The relative minus signs in beta come from the conjugated moments; they are not arbitrary sign choices for the three terms.

The complementary-corner identity, used on an actual physical H, gives `f_l²≤M(H)`. Therefore

\[
M(H)\ge\max_l f_l^2\ge
\left(\frac13\sum_l f_l\right)^2.
\]

There is no missing factor of three. Equivalently, `3Σf_l²−(Σf_l)²=Σ_(l<m)(f_l−f_m)²≥0`. Crucially, the corner whose R equals `f_l²` is the **same-row complementary corner**, not in general the mixed corner itself.

Strict D0 adds the premise `M(H)=R(E)>0`. The inequality is not an exclusion of all completions when `E` is not maximal.

### 2.3 Boxes crossing R=0

For `R(K)⊂[L,U]`, `U≤0` excludes the strict subset. Otherwise the interval

\[
Q_R=\sqrt{[\max(0,L),U]}
\]

encloses the square root at every physical strict seed in K. The rational alpha/beta functions are evaluated over all of K, with full-box denominator checks; only the square-root argument is restricted to the potentially physical subset. This does not assert that a negative-R point has a side completion.

For each sign, some known-column pair must give a squared-mean interval whose lower endpoint is strictly above U. The witness pair may differ between signs. A single physical side uses one sign for all three pairs; the implementation does not improperly choose signs separately to justify an acceptance. The transposed orientation is checked independently and may have a different sign.

The second-order jet bound is the integral Taylor remainder along the segment from center to seed. Full-box Hessian bounds and denominator separation justify it even when the center itself has negative R. No derivative of `sqrt(R)` is needed.

### 2.4 Dependencies and replay

The local result uses the proved complementary-square identity; accepted strict-side **two-orbit exhaustion**; accepted unit-lambda and symmetric-side identities; and proved transpose invariance. Their labels in the proof are accurate. The symbolic replay proves the displayed algebra conditional on those identities; it does not establish that the accepted formulas parametrize all physical sides.

Global strict-corner coverage, positive-sheet surjectivity, F6 classification, Fourier tubes, and quartet assumptions are not required for this local implication. They must not be smuggled into its interpretation as a full global exclusion.

## 3. Side-chart cover

**Verdict: gap in the stated failure-return contract; the central enclosure and injection arguments are correct.**

### 3.1 Why two validated roots per orientation cover the side fibers

For each fixed seed parameter s, the proposed phase polynomial p(s), fixed binary preconditioner A, and side-error radius define the map

\[
q\longmapsto q-A(\Phi(q)-t(s)).
\]

The componentwise inequalities `eta_i+lambda_i*r<r`, together with `max lambda_i<1`, give a self-map and contraction on the cube centered at p(s). The Jacobian enclosure covers the entire phase cube, not only its center. Since `I−A DΦ` has norm below one, `A DΦ` and therefore A are invertible; a fixed point solves the original moment equations.

This establishes a real side for every seed in the box. It does **not**, by itself, establish exhaustion. The additional full-box separation test rules out all six column permutations identifying the two validated sides. Only then does accepted two-orbit exhaustion imply that both orbits have been found. The same reasoning is applied to the transposed seed.

Four pairings must be retained, not just the normally flat pairings. The implementation retains all four. Exceptional physical pairings therefore are not dropped merely because a floating proposal carries a particular sign label.

For any physical framed H, its sides have the form `B'=BP` and `C'=Q^T C` for some representatives and permutations. The unique forced completion transforms as `D'=Q^T D P`. Hence the four representatives with their 36 independent side permutations cover all framed completions, including H+. The chosen representative/permutation may vary with the seed. No single unpermuted principal-root model is claimed to contain H+ throughout the box.

Sending only four representatives to the quartet cover is sufficient because these ordinary row/column permutations preserve the extension question. Nonflat cross-pairings only enlarge the family being tested; their presence does not supply a false physicality assertion.

### 3.2 Fixed-inverse completion and shared coordinates

Let `A_c=C*`, `F=E*B`, and choose a fixed exact binary complex proposal Y. From

\[
\|I-YA_c\|_\infty\le\lambda<1,
\qquad Z=Y(A_cP_D+F),
\]

one gets

\[
D-P_D=-(YA_c)^{-1}Z,\qquad
\max_i |(D-P_D)_{ij}|\le
\frac{\max_i|Z_{ij}|}{1-\lambda}.
\]

The residual already contains Y; an extra factor `||Y||` is not missing. The bound validates the actual working C block and does not misapply a uniform bound from a different selected chart. Adding each complex-disc radius to both Cartesian remainders is safe.

The Julia code swaps normalized variables 2 and 3 back in the vertical predictor **before** combining it with the horizontal side (`provider.jl:168–195`). This is essential and is implemented. The Python frozen completion replay uses whole-box ranges, where forgetting correlations merely widens the enclosure. Its isotropic radius makes the transposed parameter box the appropriate range.

`sc_poly` declares a new exact polynomial proposal; it does not assert that a discarded remainder has become zero for an already certified function. The subsequent residual test supplies the certification afresh.

### 3.3 Cover injection

This check passes in the patched replay against the unchanged original files.

### 3.4 Precise gap: cover-stage exceptions are outside the catch

The call to `certify_cover_with_model` at lines 219–221, and subsequent result handling, are outside that `try` block. ## 4. EffectiveConfinement §5 and the JPT theorem

### 4.1 The hypotheses have not been dropped

For integer polynomial equalities/weak inequalities defining T, degree bounded by even d, and coefficient height H also bounding the integer objective g, the theorem concerns a **compact connected component C of T**. When the attained minimum is nonzero, its absolute value is at least

\[
\left(2^{4-n/2}\widetilde H d^n\right)^{-n2^nd^n},
\qquad \widetilde H=\max(H,2n+2m).
\]

The paper uses m for the number of constraints; the repository uses s. Its setup has `n≥2` and positive even d; the proposed application satisfies both. It does not require nonsingular constraint gradients. The noncompact-component extension is unnecessary here. [JPT, Theorem 1 and §2 setup]

### 4.2 Compactness and positivity in this application

`PROOF.md:353–382` constructs each sign-chart set `T_sigma` with 25 bounded chart variables and one epigraph variable `0≤w≤48`. Positive denominators were cleared without changing the inequalities. The resulting set is closed and bounded.

There are

\[
30+50+800+2+1=883
\]

constraint polynomials, with degree at most 38 and coefficient magnitudes at most `2^34`. The objective is w. Sign choices label finitely many separate sets; they do not introduce another collection of optimization variables.

The proof separately excludes `w=0`: all signed invariants would vanish; physicality and accepted strict-corner coverage then force equivalence to F6; every fully dephased F6 equivalent has sixth-root entries, contradicting the imposed bad-entry constraint. This is the exact place where accepted strict-corner coverage enters. The JPT theorem itself is not used to prove positivity.

For a nonempty `T_sigma`, compactness gives a minimizing point. Its connected component is closed in the compact set and therefore compact. The minimum on that component equals the positive global minimum. Empty sets require no application. This meets the theorem's actual hypotheses; neither compactness nor a nonzero minimum has been inferred from the quantitative bound being proved.

### 4.3 Parameter substitution and exponent

Substitution gives

\[
n=26,\quad d=38,\quad s=883,\quad H=2^{34},
\qquad 2n+2s=1818<H.
\]

Therefore the base is `2^25 38^26 < 2^181`, and the positive exponent is `26·76^26`. With

\[
K=181\cdot26\cdot76^{26}
=37480495363891384344122334580448617578688715115462656,
\]

one obtains `w>2^(-K)`, and, on choosing the physical epigraph point `w=sqrt(M(H))`, `M(H)>2^(-2K)`. The printed N is exactly 2K. Both the strict comparison and the squaring step are correct.

The extremely small result is a practical limitation, not an erroneous application of JPT. This review has not replaced it with a production-width bound.
