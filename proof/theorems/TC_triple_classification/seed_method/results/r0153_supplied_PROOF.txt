Local completeness in a full-Hadamard phase ball of radius 0.153
27 October 2026

SCOPE AND ACCEPTANCE

The statement below concerns one exact Hadamard centre H_*, including
every completing basis K. Its computational acceptance condition is
that verify_all.py finishes with LOCAL_H_BALL_COMPLETENESS_PASS. Individual
contraction receipts alone do not establish exhaustive completeness.

The radius is 153/1000 radians in horizontal Frobenius phase norm. This
exceeds every full-Hadamard enclosure radius of the 2,775 seed records
in the pinned current atlas. It does not establish completeness at those
2,775 different centres. The final section gives the additional inclusion
and classification statements needed to apply local results to that atlas.

THEOREM

Let

       [1   1   1   1   1   1]
       [1  -1  -i   i  -i   i]
 H_* = [1  -i  -1  -i   i   i]
       [1   i  -i  -1   i  -i]
       [1  -i   i   i  -1  -i]
       [1   i   i  -i  -i  -1].

Suppose U is a real 6 by 6 matrix with zero row and column sums and
||U||_F <= R := 153/1000. Suppose H=H_* o exp(iU) is Hadamard, where
o denotes entrywise multiplication. Then every unit-entry K satisfying

 H^*H=K^*K=6I,                  |(H^*K)_{jk}|^2=6

belongs, with I and H/sqrt(6), to the known complementary-circulant
construction or the isolated Fourier/product type, up to the allowed
equivalences of triples. More precisely, the triple has an actual balanced
order-three ray symmetry at an anchor, which the recognition argument
in Section 8 of the supplied transverse CHECKPOINT.md classifies.

Any row monomial transformation of H used to enter this ball must also
act on K. Column phases and permutations of H and K may be independent.
The theorem is invariant under these transformations.

1. Exact reference data and the residual.

Use circ(a,b,c) with rows (a,b,c),(c,a,b),(b,c,a), and set

 A=circ(1,i,i), B=circ(1,1,-1), D=diag(1,-i,-i,1,1,-1).

Then H_*=D [[A,B],[B^*,-A^*]] D. Let omega=exp(2 pi i/3),
(F_3)_{rq}=omega^(rq), and Z=diag(z_0,z_1,z_2), where

 z_0=(2+i)/sqrt(5),
 z_1=((sqrt(3)-1)+i(sqrt(3)+1))/(2 sqrt(2)),
 z_2=(-(1+sqrt(3))+i(1-sqrt(3)))/(2 sqrt(2)).

Define K_*=D [[F_3,F_3],[F_3 Z,-F_3 Z]]. The exact arithmetic code
constructs 120 unbiased reference rays over Q(i,sqrt(2),sqrt(3),sqrt(5)),
checks 60 monomial symmetries of H_*, and obtains ten distinct images
of this K_*. Every reference identity is checked exactly. The numerical
root census is not assumed to exhaust all roots or all completing bases.

For H=H_* o exp(iU) and K=K_* o exp(iV), F(U,V) has 96 real entries:
the real and imaginary parts of the fifteen off-diagonal column inner
products of H, the corresponding thirty entries for K, and the 36 entries
(|(H^*K)_{jk}|^2-6)/6. Each column of V has zero mean, obtained by an
allowed scalar phase. Write J=DF(0), with blocks J_H,J_K.

2. A sharper bound on the Hadamard normal component.

Let W_H be the 25-dimensional space of horizontal H phases. Exact
rational algebra gives orthogonal projectors P_T and P_N on W_H, with
rank P_T=4 and P_T W_H the kernel of the H-orthogonality derivative.
An interval Gram calculation proves that its normal singular value is
greater than 12/5. The universal Hadamard remainder bound sqrt(3)||U||^2
therefore gives ||nu||<=3R^2/4 for nu=P_N U. Put u=P_T U.

This bound is sharpened using a rational approximate normal inverse A_N.
The only nonzero equation weights are on the thirty H equations. The
verifier retains E_N=P_N-A_N J_H W_H rather than assuming E_N=0.
It certifies ||E_N||<2.183e-11 and ||A_N||<0.410.

For Q=D^2(A_N F)(0), flatten each pair of input indices and certify the
operator norm from their tensor product to the 36-dimensional output.
Since ||a tensor b||=||a||||b||, this gives vector-valued bounds

 ||Q[u,u]|| <= 0.601 ||u||^2,
 ||Q[u,nu]|| <= 0.372 ||u||||nu||,
 ||Q[nu,nu]|| <= 1.068 ||nu||^2.

All three bounds are interval Gram inequalities. They are not obtained
from sampling directions or adding bounds on separate output entries.

The joint third-order remainder in the H equations is at most
sqrt(2/3)||U||^3. Indeed, for each column pair the complex remainder is
at most ||U_j-U_k||^3/6; the squared maximum column difference is at
most 2||U||^2 and the sum of squared differences is at most 6||U||^2.
These imply the stated joint bound after squaring and summing.

Thus, whenever ||nu||<=n, the exact residual identity improves it to

 ||nu|| <= ||E_N|| R + 0.601 R^2/2 + 0.372 R n
                 + 1.068 n^2/2 + 0.410 sqrt(2/3) R^3.       (1)

Starting with 3R^2/4, upward-rounded rational iterations give

 ||nu|| <= 0.0087738674 < n_* := 0.0088.                    (2)

An additional exact identity P_T E_j P_T=P_T/6, where E_j selects H
column j, implies the per-column bound

 ||U_j|| <= r_c := R/sqrt(6)+n_*.

These are consequences of the H equations alone, before restricting K.

3. Exhaustive localization of every completing basis.

Every ray unbiased to I has a representative
(1,exp(2 pi i beta_1),...,exp(2 pi i beta_5)). The verifier starts from
the entire closed five-dimensional phase torus and uses the necessary
Stage C inequalities at resolutions 12,24,48,96. They use R, r_c, the
four tangent directions, and the normal allowance (2). The floating
tangent frame is checked against the exact projector and its discrepancy
is added to the allowance. Stage C arguments are in cycles, so radian
phase bounds are divided by 2 pi with outward rounding.

The inherited Stage C tests use explicit conservative binary64 margins
of 1e-7, as proved in the supplied STAGEC.md and RIGOUR.md. Exponentials
come from Arb-rounded tables. The memory-bounded orthogonality routine
uses the same formulas and is compared with the inherited routine on
a fixed subset. Its proposed test directions can be arbitrary real
vectors; no inverse computed in floating point is a proof premise.

Surviving bins are assigned to labels. Each label is colored by one of
the 120 exact reference rays. This coloring is a proposal whose necessary
distance bounds are subsequently checked in Arb. The verifier checks
that there are no same-color orthogonality edges, and enumerates every
six-clique of the quotient graph. A genuine completing basis must have
one of these color patterns. A label is retained whenever at least one
pattern supplies neighbors of every other color. The disjunction over
patterns is essential; no single pattern is selected prematurely.

Every surviving pattern has two distinguished colors belonging to one
of the ten exact reference bases. Arb checks the distance of every
assigned bin of either distinguished color, including its entire
within-bin radius, to be less than 0.4 radians.

4. Contraction of the distinguished columns and exact filtering.

For a distinguished column l, use only its six mixed unbiasedness
equations. A rational approximate inverse gives an identity

 V_l=M_l eta+error,             u=T eta, ||eta||<=R.

Its inverse defect is retained. In bounding its Taylor remainder, the
carrier (T eta,M_l eta) is kept together; the normal H error and the
remaining K error are separate inputs. Each scalar Hessian block has
an interval-certified operator norm. Third-order mixed remainders use
sqrt(2)||V_l-U_j||^3/3. For the difference norm, the better of the direct
column bound and the affine bound is used. Fifty enclosing iterations,
starting from radius 0.4, give

 ||V_l||<=0.155524 < 0.157,
 ||V_l-M_l eta||<=0.0303699 < 0.032.                       (3)

For another candidate ray, let v be a bin midpoint, w a distinguished
exact ray, q_r=conj(w_r)v_r, and S=sum q_r. If this bin is orthogonal
to a ray within radius 0.157 of w, then, for any complex weight z,

 |Re(conj(z)S)| <= rho ||P_6 Im(conj(z)q)|| + |z|rho^2/2,   (4)

where P_6 subtracts the mean and rho is 0.157 plus the bin's entire
phase radius. This follows directly from the first-order exponential
Taylor formula with remainder <=t^2/2. Every new rejection using (4)
is independently checked in Arb.

A color can occur with more than one distinguished pair. The verifier
therefore keeps a separate list of possible bins for every (color,pair)
state. A six-color pattern is discarded only when one of its required
states has no possible bin. It verifies that precisely the ten exact
basis patterns remain. Their color sets are disjoint. For these states,
the remaining bins are split into their 32 children at resolution 192,
retested, and checked in Arb to satisfy

 ||V_l|| < 0.32       for each of the other four columns.    (5)

All labels, bins, quotient cliques, pair states and rejection margins
are reproducible from the supplied code. See the cover and filter
receipts for the exact counts produced in the accepted run.

5. Contraction of the remaining columns and the full basis.

For each of the other four columns, use its six unbiasedness equations
and its two complex orthogonality equations against the distinguished
columns. The affine carrier retains the correlations with both of those
columns. The inverse and linear defects are retained explicitly. The
same Hessian-block and cubic-remainder method, starting from (3),(5),
gives phase radius <=0.1541696 and affine error <=0.1091874. We keep
the simpler bounds 0.16 and 0.115.

Let P be the order-three phase action induced by p=(1,2,0,4,5,3):

 (P U)_{rj}=U_{p(r),p(j)},       (P V)_{rk}=V_{p(r),k}.

The exact reference identities show that this is a genuine common
monomial action. Split the four H tangent directions into the rank-two
fixed space T_0 and the rank-two moving space T_1. For a rational
whole-basis inverse A, put B=-A J_H P_T. Project B onto the fixed K
space and the fixed H tangent space to obtain B_0, retaining B-B_0
as a linear defect. The affine surface

 L_0 eta_0 = (T_0 eta_0, B_0 T_0 eta_0)

is exactly fixed by P; ||L_0||<1.55.

Write a candidate (U,V) as this fixed carrier plus moving H tangent,
normal H, and K carrier errors. A second approximate inverse extracts
the two moving H coordinates. The joint iteration uses the full set
of H orthogonality, K orthogonality, and mixed equations. All defects
and all four classes of Hessian cross terms are included. The accepted
50-step bounds are

 moving H norm <= 0.0120925,
 distinguished K errors <= 0.0233255 each,
 other K errors <= 0.024915 each,
 normal H norm <= 0.0088.

The squared distance to L_0 eta_0 is their squared sum. Consequently
every zero in any of the ten complete-basis enclosures lies within
0.0616024 < 0.065 of this fixed surface, with ||eta_0||<=R.

6. The fixed surface admits no symmetry-breaking zero in this tube.

Use the grid eta_0=(a/100,b/100), -15<=a,b<=15, a^2+b^2<=257.
Every eta_0 of norm <=0.153 has a nearest grid point in this set,
at distance <=sqrt(2)/200. There are 793 grid points. Every candidate
from Step 5 is therefore within

 0.065 + 1.55 sqrt(2)/200 < 0.075961 < 0.08               (6)

of a fixed reference a=L_0 eta_0 on this grid. These references need
not themselves be solutions of the MUB equations.

Let W be the joint horizontal projector

 W_((b,r,j),(d,s,k))=(delta_rs-1/6)(delta_bd delta_jk-1/12),

and E=[I-(I+P+P^2)/3]W. Exact rational algebra verifies that E is a
rank-40 orthogonal projector. At each of the 793 references, an Arb
Gram/LDL calculation proves

 ||DF(a)v||^2 > 0.9 ||v||^2     for nonzero v in range E.  (7)

The residual F has the global bound ||D^2F(x)[v,w]||<=sqrt(116)||v||||w||.
Here is a derivation that does not require x to be a solution. Set
a_j=||v_H,j||^2, b_k=||v_K,k||^2 and c_j,d_k analogously for w;
write A,B,C,D for their sums. The two orthogonality blocks contribute
at most 20(AC+BD) to the squared output norm. For a mixed equation,
its cosine expansion gives the bound
2||v_K,k-v_H,j||||w_K,k-w_H,j||. Squaring and summing gives at most
96(AC+BD)+16(AD+BC). The sum is bounded by 116(A+B)(C+D).

Suppose F(x)=0 in one of the balls (6). Then F(Px)=0, and the segment
between x and Px stays in that ball because P is orthogonal and Pa=a.
Put q=Px-x. Its joint horizontal projection Wq belongs to range E.
The difference q-Wq is an infinitesimal common-row gauge and DF(z)
annihilates it at every phase array z. The fundamental theorem of
calculus and the global Hessian bound therefore imply

 ||DF(a)Wq|| <= sqrt(116) (0.08) ||Wq||.

But 116(0.08)^2=0.7424<0.9, contradicting (7) unless Wq=0.
The gauge used here makes H horizontal and each K column mean-free.
The exact identities checked in verify_reference_balls.py include
C D C=C/2, where C is the common-row gauge projector and D projects
onto this chosen gauge. Since q is in range D and Wq=0 forces q in
range C, this identity forces q=0. Thus Px=x exactly.

On K_* the associated common monomial action has eigenvalues
1,omega,omega^2, each twice. Px=x preserves this actual balanced
assignment on K, and makes the other two bases monomial under the
same action. Recognition therefore gives the asserted known type.
The exact symmetries from Step 1 transfer the conclusion to every
one of the ten completing-basis patterns. This proves the theorem
when the stated computational acceptance condition has passed.

The local theorem above cannot be moved to an arbitrary atlas centre
by changing the name of H_*. To apply it to a source cell, one must
certify its inclusion after an allowed equivalence. A sufficient bound
is d_j+rho_j<=0.153, where d_j bounds the horizontal phase distance
between its centre and an equivalent H_* and rho_j is the transported
full-H radius. Direct bounds on the entire affine enclosure can improve
this triangle bound. Alternatively, establish an analogous local
certificate at another known centre, or exclude all third bases on
that cell. Those inclusions and other-centre classifications are not
provided by this local theorem.

The full conjecture still requires coverage of every surviving triple
cell, the Fourier chart, and Tao. No global triple-completeness PASS
may be inferred from the radius comparison or from the no-quartet
receipt. The original user-supplied brief explicitly describes its
triple-neighborhood cell list as work in progress.
