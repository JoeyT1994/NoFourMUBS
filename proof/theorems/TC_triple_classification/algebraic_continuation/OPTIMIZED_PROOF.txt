Optimized scalar exclusion on the original balls

We retain the complete ray cover, color patterns, conditional contractions,
and original radii of the frozen native certifier. The preceding algebraic
release supplies a signed quadratic lower bound for y.F on each correlated
tube. A positive lower bound excludes that tube. The seven nonnegative
dual multipliers and the residual direction y are exact binary64 inputs
to the Arb verifier. Neither numerical optimization nor a solver's status
establishes the inequality.

Each column has two bounds with the same phase center v_c:
  ||V_c-v_c-T_c w|| <= epsilon_c,
  ||V_c-v_c|| <= p_c.
For a difference of K columns, its phase displacement is therefore at
most the minimum of ||T_b-T_a|| r+epsilon_a+epsilon_b and p_a+p_b.
For a mixed H/K column difference it is at most the minimum of
||T_b-U_a|| r+epsilon_b+n_a and ||U_a|| r+n_a+p_b.
Here U_a is the mean-zero H column frame and n_a bounds its nonlinear
remainder. Both alternatives hold on the same conditional zero enclosure.
Taking their minimum is valid. These improved bounds replace only the
cubic remainder radii in the preceding signed quadratic test.

The conic optimizer proposes y and all seven multipliers together. It
uses floating-point models and may fail or give an inaccurate answer.
Acceptance recomputes the residual, Jacobian, Hessian, remainder, interval
LDL positive-definiteness test, and inverse expression in Arb. A fresh
replay reconstructs the entire original all-K cover and checks the saved
direction and multipliers without rerunning the conic optimizer.

The wrapper applies this test only after the existing pattern tests fail.
It records every failed bound, source change, resource cap, and timeout as
unresolved. No original radius is reduced. Conditional zero covers do not
become covers of positive residual levels. A full classification still
requires every original ball and the exceptional cases to be certified.
