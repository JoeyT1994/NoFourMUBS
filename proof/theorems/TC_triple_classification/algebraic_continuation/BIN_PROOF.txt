Quadratic filtering of the original closed column-phase bins

Fix one original native H-ball and one of the exhaustive column bins.
The first entry of a column ray is fixed to one. Its other five phases
have midpoint 2 pi k_j/192 and half-width pi/96. Subtracting the mean
phase gives beta+G t, with |t_j|<=1 and G equal to pi/96 times the
mean-zero projection of the five coordinate vectors. In particular,
||G t|| <= sqrt(29/6) pi/96. These are the same closed bins retained by
the native Stage C cover. The seed coordinates satisfy ||w/r||<=1.

For each H column a we retain the six unbiasedness residuals
  f_a = (|H_a^* v|^2-6)/6
      = (1/3) sum_{i<j} cos(V_i-V_j-Q_ia+Q_ja).
At the bin midpoint, Taylor expansion retains the full signed quadratic
in the nine variables (w/r,t_1,...,t_5). The known native H2 term is
included in its seed-seed block. The linear X z term is combined across
the six equations before its norm is taken.

Let a_a=||U_a|| r+sqrt(29/6) pi/96 and
n_a=||H2_a|| r^2/2+||X_a|| tau. The H columns are projected to mean-zero
phases. The remaining scalar error for equation a is at most
  2 a_a n_a+n_a^2+sqrt(2)(a_a+n_a)^3/3.
The Hessian bound 2 and the cubic bound sqrt(2)/3 follow directly from
sum_{i<j}(d_i-d_j)^2=6||d||^2 and |d_i-d_j|<=sqrt(2)||d|| for mean-zero d.
For a residual direction y, we add the norm of its combined X-linear
coefficient times tau and sum the displayed errors with weights |y_a|.

The retained polynomial is minimized over one four-dimensional unit
Euclidean ball and five one-dimensional unit balls. The preceding
quadratic dual applies with six nonnegative multipliers. Arb checks its
positive-definiteness inequality, inverse expression, and strict scalar
margin. Only a verified positive margin removes a bin. Conic optimization
merely proposes the six residual weights and six multipliers.

Filtering occurs after the original column tests have produced an
exhaustive color-clique list. Retaining the union of the edges of those
cliques cannot introduce a clique absent from that exhaustive list, and
does not remove an actual completing basis. The new filter then removes
only certified impossible bins. All remaining bins, including untested
ones and every unsuccessful numerical proposal, remain in the cover.
The original closed H-ball and its radius remain unchanged.

Replay reconstructs the native cover and its first column contractions,
checks every saved excluded bin and multiplier, rejects duplicate or
unrecognized bin identifiers, and then rebuilds the complete basis cover.
Its acceptance never depends on saved margin values or a solver status.
