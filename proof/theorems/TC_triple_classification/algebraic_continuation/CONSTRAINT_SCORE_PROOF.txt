Removing vanishing equations from the transition-phase bound

The column cover gives phase bounds for every completing basis, but it
also covers phase arrays that do not satisfy the triple equations. We
can use those equations without first contracting every column further.
Let F(q) be the 96 real triple equations in moving_symmetry.py, with the
36 phases of H followed by the 36 phases of K. Every completing triple
satisfies F(q)=0. All phase centers, gauges and displacement bounds below
are those in UNITARY_SCORE_PROOF.txt.

Write q=q0+delta and let Lc be the transition matrix at q0. Every entry of
Lc must be nonzero for this test. For each entry set zeta=conj(Lc)/|Lc|.
Define S(q)=Im(zeta L(q)) entrywise, and let W remove row and column means.
Then S(q0)=0 exactly. For an actual triple, S(q)=sin(theta), where theta
is the principal entrywise phase difference from Lc. The preceding
unit-modulus estimate bounds both ||theta|| by E and every |theta| by b.
Consequently ||W(theta-sin(theta))|| <= b^2 E/6.

Choose any fixed real matrix A of size 36 by 96 and put
  G(q)=W S(q)-A F(q).
At a completing triple G(q)=W sin(theta). At the center G(q0)=-A F(q0).
Taylor's formula therefore gives
  W theta + A F(q0)
    = DG(q0) delta + (1/2) D2G(q0)[delta,delta]
      + R3 + W(theta-sin(theta)).
The implementation chooses A numerically to reduce the K part of DG,
then treats every entry of A as an exact binary64 number. It rebuilds
F, its derivatives, the derivative defect and the signed Hessian in Arb.
No rank assertion or numerical inverse is accepted as a proof step.

The H displacement is U w+n, with ||w||<=r. Its remainder n includes
H2[w,w]/2, X xi and the radius of the H center. The K displacement z has
six column bounds p_k and hence ||z||<=p=sqrt(sum p_k^2). For the linear
term, keep DG_H U, DG_H H2 and DG_H X as signed products. Bound DG_K z by
the smaller of its whole operator bound and the sum of its six column
group bounds. The uncertainty of the H center is bounded separately.

For the Hessian, regard each bilinear output as a linear map on a tensor
product. The Euclidean norm of x tensor y is ||x|| ||y||. The six signed
maps on (w,w), (w,z), (z,z), (w,n), (z,n) and (n,n) therefore give a bound
with coefficients r^2/2, rp, p^2/2, r||n||, p||n|| and ||n||^2/2.
Their operator bounds are verified by the inherited Arb Gram test.

For real t, the remainder of exp(it) after degree two has modulus at
most |t|^3/6. If h_a bounds the phase displacement of H column a, the
complex remainder in an H orthogonality equation is at most
(h_a+h_b)^3/6. A K orthogonality equation similarly has remainder at most
(p_a+p_b)^3/6. The mixed equation for columns a and k is a sum of pair
cosines divided by three. Its remainder is at most
  (sqrt(2)/3)(h_a+p_k)^3.
Indeed, sum_(i<j)(d_i-d_j)^2=6||hor(d)||^2 and
max_(i<j)|d_i-d_j|<=sqrt(2)||hor(d)||.
For S itself, the entrywise remainder is at most
(h_a+p_k)^3/(6 sqrt(6)). Combine these bounds in Euclidean norm, using
either ||A|| or the sum of its two-column complex blocks and single
mixed-equation columns. Projection by W cannot enlarge the S remainder.

Let E_G be the sum of the resulting linear, quadratic, cubic and sine
error bounds. With P=arg(Lc)-A F(q0), the displayed Taylor identity
implies ||W(arg(L)-P)||<=E_G, using principal entrywise lifts from Lc.
The Fourier score is invariant under the choices of lifts. Thus
  Gamma_F(L) <= Gamma_F(exp(iP)) + (2/5) E_G.
The center score is evaluated in Arb at a proposed alignment, as before.
The matrix A need not be horizontal: applying W to the displayed error
can only reduce its norm. We retain the preceding unsigned upper bound
and select the smaller valid result.

The quadratic term can be reduced by another multiple of F. Let J=DF(q0),
let T_o be the Hessian of output o of G, and choose any fixed real matrix
B of size 72 by 96. Put P0=I-BJ and V=(I+P0)/2. For delta=q-q0, subtract
  M_o(q) = T_o[V delta, B F(q)]
from G_o(q). This changes nothing at a completing triple because F(q)=0.
At the center M_o is zero. The new derivative is
  DG_o(q0) - (V^T T_o B F(q0))^T,
and the new Hessian is P0^T T_o P0. To check the latter identity, expand
P0^T T_o P0 and use V=I-BJ/2. No invertibility of J is required.

Write rho_F=F(q)-F(q0)-J delta. The additional remainder is exactly
  -T_o[V delta, B rho_F].
We bound rho_F without assuming that q is a triple. For each complex
Gram equation its first-order Taylor remainder has modulus at most half
the squared norm of the corresponding column phase difference. For a
mixed equation, let m bound that phase difference. Its remainder is at
most m^2. If M is its signed 6 by 6 Hessian at the center, a second valid
bound is ||M|| m^2/2+(sqrt(2)/3)m^3. Keep the smaller of these two bounds.
The Euclidean norm of the 30 complex and 36 real remainder bounds then
bounds ||rho_F|| in the original 96 real coordinates.

To bound T[V delta,B rho_F], retain its three signed tensor maps for
delta=(U w,0)+(0,z)+(n,0). Their operator bounds are multiplied by r, p
and ||n||, then by the bound for ||rho_F||. A second bound treats the H
Gram, K Gram and mixed residual blocks separately and sums their bounds.
We keep the smaller result and add it to the preceding cubic remainder.
The revised derivative and Hessian replace the preceding ones in the
linear and quadratic estimates. The constant -A F(q0) is unchanged.
Thus the same score inequality applies to G-M.

The implementation proposes the lower 36 rows of B from a truncated
numerical pseudoinverse of the K Jacobian and sets its other rows to zero.
It treats the proposed entries as exact binary64 numbers and recomputes
P0, V, the derivative correction, Hessian and remainder bounds in Arb.
The pseudoinverse is a way to choose B, not a rank or inverse certificate.

We can also keep the direction of the linear phase error when bounding
the score. Write that error as D x, where the first four entries of x
are w/r and the other six groups are the K-column displacements divided
by their respective bounds p_k. Each of these seven groups has norm at
most one. A zero radius gives a zero block of D and requires no division
in the implementation. Keep the remaining H displacement, the quadratic
term, the cubic remainder and the sine error in a bound E_rest.

Fix any Fourier alignment and winding integers for the center P, and
let d be its vector of 225 plaquette residuals. Let R map 36 entry phases
to those plaquettes. The identity R^T R=36 W gives
  ||d+R D x||^2/225 = c + g^T x + x^T Q x/2,
where
  c=||d||^2/225,  g=2 D^T R^T d/225,  Q=(8/25) D^T W D.
Apply the existing product-of-balls quadratic lower bound to -Q and -g.
If it returns the verified lower bound ell, then u=c-ell is an upper
bound for the displayed squared norm. The winding integers need not
minimize the score away from the center, since any fixed choice gives
an upper bound. We therefore have
  Gamma_F(L) <= sqrt(u) + (2/5) E_rest.
The dual multipliers are numerical proposals, but the interval positive
definiteness and completed-square bound are checked in Arb. A failed
dual supplies no bound. We keep the smaller of this result and the
preceding bounds, so the additional calculation cannot weaken them.

This is a necessary bound for every completing triple in the supplied
tubes. It does not establish the Fourier-neighbourhood theorem or full
ball coverage. Those dependencies and independent replay are still
required before promoting a score calculation to a classification.
