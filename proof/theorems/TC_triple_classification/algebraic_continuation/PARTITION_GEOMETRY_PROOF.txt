Enclosing the intersection with the original closed ball

Let B be the original ball ||w||<=r, and let D be a closed dyadic box.
For a fixed center c and any 0<=lambda<=1,
  ||w-c||^2
  <= lambda r^2+||c||^2
     +max_{v in D} ((1-lambda)||v||^2-2 c^T v),   w in B intersect D.
This follows by replacing lambda ||w||^2 by lambda r^2. The polynomial
inside the maximum is a sum of convex one-coordinate quadratics. Each
coordinate maximum occurs at an endpoint, so the right-hand side is
computed by four pairs of endpoint evaluations. Arb bounds every term.
We may take the smallest of these verified upper bounds over finitely
many proposed centers and multipliers.

We use the original radial projection of the box midpoint, the box point
nearest the origin, their midpoint, and half the radial projection. The
nearest point belongs to B whenever the exact dyadic outside test has not
excluded D. The remaining candidates belong to B by radial projection
and convexity. Thus each selected center is valid for the original
normal-form center-jet construction. The multiplier is chosen from
0,1/8,...,1. A zero-radius boundary intersection is retained.

The local phase model uses Q(c), DQ(c), the original quadratic phase term,
and the original verified bound Z2 on the second derivative of its normal
remainder. Its local normal error is at most Z2 rho^2/2. This is a
conditional restriction to B intersect D. Taylor segments stay inside
the convex original ball, and no continuation of the chart outside that
ball is assumed. The K cover is restricted only by necessary tests on
this conditional enclosure.

The dyadic tree retains all closed child boxes and rejects a box as
outside only when its exact nearest-point squared distance exceeds r^2.
Every non-outside leaf must be excluded or classified, and the tree
checker must verify every branch, before a complete partition can prove
the original domain. A failed cell, exhausted cell limit, or partial
pattern run is unresolved. This improvement changes local bounds inside
the partition; it does not change the original radius being proved.
