Fourier-score bounds from unitary transition matrices

Let H and K have unit-modulus entries and let H/sqrt(6), K/sqrt(6) and
L=H* K/sqrt(6) define an MUB triple. Thus H/sqrt(6) and K/sqrt(6) are
unitary and every entry of L has modulus one. A star denotes adjoint.
The exhaustive column cover and the original H normal form supply the
phase enclosures used below. All conclusions concern zeros in that cover.

We may subtract the mean phase of each column of H. This multiplies H
on the right by a diagonal unitary and L on the left by its adjoint.
The labelled Fourier score is invariant under those phases. Apply the
same mean-zero projection to Q0, U, H2 and X. Take exact dyadic midpoints
of the resulting center balls; include their radii in every displacement
bound. The complex centers Hc and Kc then have exactly unit-modulus entries.

For Q=Q0+Uw+H2[w,w]/2+X xi, ||w||<=r and ||xi||<=tau, the operator norm
bound on the full 36-row maps gives
  ||H-Hc||_F <= ||U|| r + ||H2|| r^2/2 + ||X|| tau + center_radius = dH.
The exponential is 1-Lipschitz on real phases. We may also use the norm
of six independently bounded column displacements, taking the smaller
of the two valid bounds. Each column displacement is denoted h_a.

Stack the six K slope maps into a 36 by 4 matrix T. If p_k bounds the
absolute phase error of column k and epsilon_k bounds its correlated
error, then
  ||K-Kc||_F <= min(sqrt(sum p_k^2), ||T|| r + sqrt(sum epsilon_k^2)) = dK,
with the center radii included. All norms refer to the same shared w.
For the Fourier score of K the slope map may additionally be projected
onto arrays with zero row and column means: this projection removes only
gauge phases and has norm one. Its action on the error cannot enlarge it.

Put sc=||Hc||op/sqrt(6) and tc=||Kc||op/sqrt(6), bounded using their Gram
defects. Two exact identities give
  L-Lc = Hc*(K-Kc)/sqrt(6) + (H-Hc)*K/sqrt(6),
  L-Lc = H*(K-Kc)/sqrt(6) + (H-Hc)*Kc/sqrt(6),
where Lc=Hc*Kc/sqrt(6). Multiplication by an actual unitary preserves the
Frobenius norm. Consequently
  ||L-Lc||_F <= min(sc dK+dH, dK+tc dH) = D.
The H error is counted once in this whole-matrix bound. For each entry
(a,k), Cauchy-Schwarz and the exact sqrt(6) norms of the relevant columns
also give |(L-Lc)_(a,k)| <= p_k+h_a. Each entry is bounded by D as well.

The unit modulus of an actual L entry yields a sharper angle inequality.
For |t|=1, c!=0 and theta=arg(t)-arg(c) reduced to [-pi,pi],
  |t-c|^2 = (|c|-1)^2 + 4|c| sin^2(theta/2).
Let m be a positive lower bound for every |Lc_ak| and let e bound every
entry displacement. If x=e/(2 sqrt(m))<1, monotonicity of asin(x)/x gives
  |theta_ak| <= [asin(x)/(x sqrt(m))]
                sqrt(|L_ak-Lc_ak|^2-(|Lc_ak|-1)^2).
The ratio is interpreted as one at x=0. If R2 is a lower bound for the
sum of the radial terms (|Lc_ak|-1)^2, summation gives
  ||arg L-arg Lc||_F <= [asin(x)/(x sqrt(m))] sqrt(D^2-R2).
If D^2<R2 strictly, no completing triple exists in this tube. An interval
containing zero never supports that exclusion. A zero center entry or
an inconclusive angle bound leaves this L-score test unavailable.

For the labelled Fourier score, the plaquette map is B tensor B, where
B is the oriented incidence matrix of the complete graph on six vertices.
Since B*B=6(I-J/6), its norm on horizontal phase arrays is six. Circular
distance is 1-Lipschitz. After the defining factor 1/15, the score is
therefore (2/5)-Lipschitz in horizontal Frobenius norm. Its minimum over
alignments is bounded above by any single alignment. The numerical search
only chooses labels and integer windings; the chosen cost is rebuilt in
Arb. Add (2/5) of the appropriate phase-error bound to obtain the K or L
score bound. Every arithmetic acceptance comparison uses outward bounds.

An upper bound at most 19/50 can be used only together with the separately
proved Fourier-neighbourhood theorem and its listed dependencies. This
module does not establish that theorem, discharge an ordering condition,
or certify a whole ball from one pattern. Whole-ball certification still
requires all patterns, the original radius, the complete cover and an
independent replay. The radial contradiction requires no Fourier theorem.
