Excluding phantom patterns while retaining the original phase ball

A six-color pattern is a necessary condition for a completing basis K.
It does not assert that its six columns can be chosen simultaneously.
In particular, separate edges of the color graph can be witnessed by
incompatible fine bins. We call a surviving necessary pattern a phantom
only after an exclusion proves that it contains no completing triple.
A nonzero least-squares residual is useful for proposing a certificate,
but neither that residual nor a failed numerical search proves exclusion.

The new replayer treats one specified color pattern on one dyadic cell
of the original four-parameter H ball. The original atlas seed and radius
are checked against the input. The cell is a conditional restriction of
the original normal form, as in PARTITION_GEOMETRY_PROOF.txt. It is not a
replacement for the original ball. Closing a ball still requires every
original pattern and a complete cover of its H domain.

1. Keep the original fine bins in the compatibility test

First rebuild the original C1 enclosure, the normal form, and the complete
K cover. Recheck the saved quadratic bin exclusions before restricting
the surviving bins to the specified H cell. The arguments passed to
Stage C on that cell must satisfy its input hypotheses: finite arrays,
frame entries of modulus at most one, the stated radii in [0,1), and the
center operator bound below 1.01. The replayer checks these at the actual
local call site as well as checking the original ball's preconditions.

The inherited fine_patterns.refine evaluates the necessary pair tests
on the original closed bins, using the local H enclosure. It then removes
bins with no possible partner in another required color. It repeats this
removal and, when needed, searches the remaining graph exhaustively for
six-cliques. An actual basis chooses a bin in each of its six colors.
All fifteen pairs of these bins must survive the necessary pair tests.
None of its six bins can therefore be removed by this propagation, and
the selected bins form a surviving six-clique. Thus a completed empty
search excludes every completing basis of this pattern in this H cell.
A bin cap, edge cap or search cap gives no exclusion. The fine-all option
repeats this check for every pattern in the rebuilt original cover. It
excludes all K on that H cell only if every pattern is excluded.

This calculation does not identify a successful clique with a triple.
It only removes patterns for which no compatible clique remains. The
binary64 bounds in these pair tests are inherited from Stage C; their
correctness and the original all-K cover remain dependencies. No new
numerical root-completeness assumption is introduced.

2. Subtract multiples of the full triple equations

For a pattern that survives, let q contain the 36 phases of H and the 36
phases of K. Let F(q) be the 96 real equations in moving_symmetry.py.
Its Gram equations are unscaled; its mixed equations are
(|H_a^* K_c|^2-6)/6. Every completing triple satisfies F(q)=0.

Choose a fixed real row y and a center q0, which need not be a zero.
Write delta=q-q0, f=F(q0), J=DF(q0), and
  T = D2(y F)(q0).
Choose any fixed 72 by 96 real matrix B. In this implementation its H
rows are zero. Define
  P = I-BJ,       V = (I+P)/2,
  G(q) = y F(q) - T[V delta, B F(q)].
The subtracted term vanishes whenever F(q)=0, so G(q)=0 at every actual
triple. This remains true for an inaccurate numerical choice of B.

At q0 the value is c=y f. Its derivative and Hessian are
  j_G = y J - (V^T T B f)^T,
  T_G = P^T T P.
Indeed, with A=BJ, subtracting the quadratic term changes T to
T-(I-A/2)^T T A-A^T T(I-A/2)=(I-A)^T T(I-A).
The linear correction containing f must be kept because q0 need not
be a zero. The tests include this identity at a nonzero residual.

Put rho_F=F(q)-f-J delta. Taylor's formula gives the exact decomposition
  G(q) = c + j_G delta + (1/2) delta^T T_G delta
         + y R3_F - T[V delta, B rho_F].
Here R3_F is the remainder of F after degree two. No inverse identity
or numerical stationarity condition has been substituted into this
formula. When BJ nearly removes the K directions, P^T T P can be much
smaller than T. The last two terms still need explicit bounds.

3. Enclose the entire supplied tube

Use the same correlated displacement as scalar_support.py:
  delta = L w + (0,e) + (n,0),
  ||w|| <= r,  ||e_c|| <= epsilon_c,
  n = H2[w,w]/2 + X xi,  ||xi|| <= tau.
Write n_H=||H2||r^2/2+||X||tau and e_*=sqrt(sum epsilon_c^2).
Let D be the scaled map from the product of seven unit balls to
Lw+(0,e). The quadratic polynomial retained for G has constant c,
linear coefficient D^T j_G^T, and Hessian
  D^T T_G D,
with j_G,H H2[w,w]/2 added to its four seed-coordinate entries.
The remaining linear normal term is bounded by ||j_G,H X|| tau.
The quadratic terms containing n are bounded by the signed operator
norms of L^T T_G,H, the projected K-H block, and the H-H block,
with respective factors r n_H, e_* n_H and n_H^2/2.

Let h_ab, k_cd and m_ac bound the mean-zero column phase differences
in the H Gram, K Gram and mixed equations on this supplied tube.
For a complex Gram equation, the degree-two remainder has modulus at
most h_ab^3/6 or k_cd^3/6. For a mixed equation it is at most
(sqrt(2)/3)m_ac^3. Multiply these bounds by the corresponding real or
complex-block weights of y and sum them to bound y R3_F.

For the first-order remainder rho_F, the corresponding bounds are
h_ab^2/2, k_cd^2/2 and m_ac^2. Pair the real and imaginary parts of each
Gram equation when taking the Euclidean norm. These bounds require only
unit-modulus entries, not that q is a solution. The mixed bound follows
from the scalar Hessian bound two in the inherited column calculation.

Set M=V^T T B. The last remainder is delta^T M rho_F. For any chosen
residual block, its operator coefficient is bounded by
  ||L^T M_block|| r
    + ||P_K M_K,block|| e_* + ||M_H,block|| n_H,
where P_K is the exact column-mean projection. Multiply this coefficient
by the block norm bound for rho_F. The implementation takes the smaller
of the whole-residual bound and the sum of the H Gram, K Gram and mixed
block bounds. Add this to the preceding remainders to obtain E.

The inherited product-of-balls dual supplies a lower bound ell for the
retained nonconstant quadratic polynomial. It checks positive definiteness
in Arb and evaluates the completed-square bound with the proposed
nonnegative multipliers. Consequently
  c + ell - E > 0
implies G(q)>0 throughout the supplied tube, which contradicts G(q)=0
at every triple. This is a zero-exclusion certificate. Because G was
changed by a multiple of F, its margin is not advertised as a uniform
positive lower bound on ||F|| over a larger domain.

The vectors y, the entries of B and the dual multipliers are stored as
exact binary64 proposals. Replay rebuilds all coefficients and remainder
bounds in Arb. Invalid dimensions, nonfinite proposals and failed strict
inequalities reject the certificate. The K-label tree partitions both
children of every split; every unresolved leaf remains explicitly open.

The fine-bin route and the algebraic route exclude zeros. They do not
use the Fourier-neighborhood theorem, the transition-ordering rule, or
an assertion that every numerical fit is of known type. The compact
checkpoint records verified pattern cells and branches. It records no
additional whole-ball classification unless the full original-ball
coverage has separately been completed and replayed.
