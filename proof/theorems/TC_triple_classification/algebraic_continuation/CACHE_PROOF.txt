Reusing checked proposals without trusting cached conclusions

Long discovery runs can stop before a complete ball certificate is
written. The cache retains successful bin witnesses and completed cell
results so these computations need not be proposed again. Its contents
are inputs to verification, not accepted proof conclusions.

For bin reuse, the original native cover and first column contractions
are reconstructed. A saved bin is matched by its exact five integer
midpoint coordinates to the current colors. The original quadratic bin
verifier then checks every saved direction and multiplier on the current
original H model before deleting that bin. Bin exclusion depends on its
phase box and H model, not on the numerical color assigned to it. Fresh
deletions are checked by the same verifier. The final receipt contains
the complete list of reused and fresh witnesses.

For cell reuse, the original box-ball intersection, local chart enclosure,
and necessary K filters are reconstructed. The native final verifier runs
again, using a saved scalar witness only after its inequality is rechecked
on the current local tube. If that replay fails, discovery runs normally.
A saved status or a saved margin cannot exclude or classify a cell.

The complete dyadic tree and every original basis pattern remain required.
Cached work does not change the global success condition. The independent
replayer does not consult the cache: the final full-ball receipt contains
all bin witnesses and all partition leaves needed for fresh verification.
An interrupted last cache line is ignored, retaining the corresponding
domain for renewed computation. A corrupt earlier line is an error.
