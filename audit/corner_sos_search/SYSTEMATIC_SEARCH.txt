Finite combination search, 16 October 2026
==========================================

There is no finite exhaustive search over every possible polynomial degree,
multiplier, sum-of-squares support and power of the vanishing target. There is
also no benefit in solving every subset of a fixed linear multiplier pool:
the full span includes every subset by setting unused coefficients to zero.
This observation concerns the chosen mathematical ansatz. A numerical SDP
solver is not an exact decision procedure for its feasibility.

The next bounded experiment reuses the exact model AND saved numerical vector
from run 38902440868. It remains restricted to primary 000 and auxiliary 000;
all six transitions are assumed non-Tao. Other sectors are not discharged.

1. Enumerate every pair in a precisely defined finite product pool

Let B be the saved first Gram basis, T the saved target's Laurent support,
and P = {0} union B union (-B) union T union (-T). For every existing equation
f, consider every shift in P union (-support(f)). Reconstruct the COMPLETE
real Laurent multiple Re(z^shift f), including exact conjugate cancellations.
Retain the row exactly when every remaining term belongs to the saved moment
coordinates. Record all rejected products that require more moments. No
arbitrary row cap or numerical residual threshold omits an admissible row.
Canonical integer normalization removes exact duplicates and scalar multiples.

All equations are supplied by the existing adapters: raw quartet,
classification, Fourier, rational transition, transport, and the reused
seven-cofactor moment-norm identity. The archived PhysicalMomentBound source
was additionally read. Its local seed-ball and explicit cancellation/budget
hypotheses prevent its adoption as a global quartet equation. No new theorem
or unproved complementary equality is introduced.

2. Try the cheaper exact linear problem first

Seek -target as a rational linear combination of the available equation
rows. An off-target monomial occurring in only one remaining row forces that
row's coefficient to zero; peel such rows and discard components not touching
the target. This pruning is exact for PURE IDEAL MEMBERSHIP ONLY. SOS terms
could cancel those monomials, so it must never be used to rule out an SOS.

The existing audit/sos_linear.py::combination performs rational elimination.
Its attempt is limited to 45 seconds and 2 GiB. Timeout/resource failure is
unresolved, not a negative result. Any returned combination is sent through
the existing independent verify_exact.py reconstruction before acceptance.
No duplicate elimination or certificate checker is implemented.

3. Search the enlarged SOS model once

If the exact linear attempt does not succeed and new rows exist, retain ALL
of them and reuse every original moment and Gram block. Run the existing
solver for at most 300 seconds on GitHub. If no new rows exist, skip an
identical solve. The old candidate's violation of each new row is diagnostic
information; a nonzero residual is not a counterexample or a proof.

4. Let the result choose the next enlargement

A significant bound improvement supports extending the same strategy to
the remaining sectors. If the bound persists, the saved rejected-product
counts and violated relations help select joint Gram blocks/new moments.
Before repeating a solver run, make that genuine enlargement explicit.
Exact kernel/quotient reduction can improve conditioning, but alone does not
strengthen an equivalent exact relaxation. A later degree hierarchy may also
need higher even powers of Im Psi for a real-radical certificate; the present
fixed-square ansatz is not a completeness claim.

An accepted certificate must have exact rational coefficient equality and
exactly nonnegative square weights, with its classification hypotheses stated.
A single certificate directly in the original raw equations additionally
needs algebraic cofactors for the classification/Fourier theorem inputs.
Final Lean verification remains on GitHub. No atlas work is performed.
