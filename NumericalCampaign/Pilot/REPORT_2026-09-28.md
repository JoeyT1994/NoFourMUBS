## What the hard cells are

- **Folds are not the problem so far.** Every hard cell probed (g1, g3, and
  the failed pilot cell) keeps a constant ray count across the cell.
  Earlier, the Python pilot's cluster counts were wrong: g1 and g3 have 52
  rays, not 53 and 54.
- **Near-miss ghosts are the problem.** A ghost is a complex pair of
  solutions just off the real torus: |F| ≈ 0.03–0.15, σ₅ ≈ 0.03–0.08, and a
  valley about 0.02–0.035 long. Box-by-box exclusion needs about 10⁶ tiny
  boxes there.
- **Occasional ray-certificate failures.** One remaining cell fails even at
  Taylor order 6. The cause is not yet diagnosed.
- **A loose H₊ Taylor model.** This was found at 05:10 in g3. Near dF_v ≈ 0
  the side square root is small and the order-8 series converge slowly. The
  H₊ remainder is 4.7·10⁻³, which puts a floor under exclusion and
  regularity. **Fixed:** hcell now raises the order to 12 when the remainder
  exceeds 10⁻⁴, giving 1.7·10⁻⁵ in 2.7 s.
- **The X(α) family is special.** X₆ has 60 rays, and nearby carriers have
  56. It sits inside a thin tube bounded by a genuine fold wall
  ([FOLDS.md](../FOLDS.md)). One pilot cell had 60 rays.

## What I recommend next

1. **Make the search efficient around ghosts** until the failed pilot cell
   and g3 certify, then rerun the pilot. Nodes that tubes will cover should
   be sized to fit a tube directly instead of being bisected down to it.
   Tubes should be placed along the whole valley at once. Expected effect:
   hard cells cost minutes instead of hours.
2. **Diagnose the remaining ray-certificate failure.** It happens even at
   order 6.
3. **Decide the X(α) strategy.** Either build a fold certificate (the
   Lyapunov–Schmidt machinery with two roots, the same code path as ghosts)
   or use a dedicated local exclusion near X(α), which is close to MMVW
   Conjecture 3.
4. Then run a pilot on about 10⁴ cells, the 1% budget step, before the full
   campaign.
