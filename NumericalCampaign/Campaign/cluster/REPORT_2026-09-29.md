## 4. T2 measurement

**Method.** Seed distance of cell centres to the exact Fourier-family seed atlas
(`ProvenResults/FourierNeighbourhood/SeedAtlas`, exact rationals from the Float64 centres), together with
the R range and D₀ ∩ F membership at 81 float sample points per cell (evidence, not proof).
Scripts: `NumericalCampaign/Campaign/analysis/`.

d_Four (the matrix distance) is **not** estimated. A reliable value needs a global minimisation over row
and column permutations, diagonal phases and the family parameters. A local float optimiser could
overstate the distance badly, so it would mislead.

**Baseline: the raw branch layer** (2,000 random cells, before the layer prune).
- Centre distance to the atlas: median 0.37 rad; 10th percentile 0.036; 90th percentile 0.64.
- Shares within a given distance: 0.1% within 0.0061 (one cell half-width), 1.5% within 0.012,
  13% within 0.05, 19% within 0.1.

**Baseline: work cells** (2,000 random cells).
- Centre distance to the atlas: median 0.38 rad.
- **1.7% lie within one cell diameter (0.012 rad)** of the Fourier family, about 1.5·10⁵ cells.
  These are the work cells most likely to be hard to certify. The 1% sample measures their fate.

**Layer-prune survivors** (uniform sample of 8,000, taken from the first ~30% of layer tasks; evidence
in `WhatWeWanttoProve/evidence/layer_survivors_T2_*`).

| | cells | atlas distance of centre, 10/50/90% / max (rad) |
|---|---|---|
| touching D₀ ∩ F at a float sample | **202 (2.5%)** | 0.0065 / **0.021** / 0.062 / 0.093 |
| not touching | 7,798 | 0.021 / 0.095 / 0.31 / 0.81 |

- **R at the sampled D₀ points** (per-cell minimum): minimum 5.0, median 39. Only 33 of the 202 have
  a D₀ point with R < 20.
- **R = 0 crossings:** 55% of all survivors have a sampled R range containing 0.
- **Scaled to the campaign:** survivors are about 4.4% of 14.0M layer cells, about 6·10⁵. Of those,
  about 1.5·10⁴ touch D₀.

**Judgement on δ₀.**
- The D₀-touching residual lies near the Fourier family but not on it. Its D₀ points are at seed
  distance ~0.02 rad (median) from the family, with **moderate R** (median 39).
- A matrix tube of δ₀ = 1/8192, or the now-proved 1/16384 (~10⁻⁴), cannot absorb these cells. Their D₀
  points are ~2 orders of magnitude farther away in seed distance. Near the family the
  seed-to-carrier map is not contracting (√-type behaviour near R = 0), so the matrix distances are
  not smaller.
- To remove the whole D₀-touching residual by a tube alone would need a seed radius of about
  0.1 rad. That is matrix radius O(10⁻¹), roughly 1000× the proved radius. This is not realistic
  as a theorem target.
- **What is realistic:**
  1. Since R ≥ 5 at the residual D₀ points, bisect these ~1.5·10⁴ cells and certify their sub-cells
     with the rays or cover certificates. RegularSideCharts shows the reconstruction is regular for
     R > 0, and the production constructor succeeds on sub-cells.
  2. Discard their R ≤ 1 parts with the ResidualCells R-gap lemma, once it is generalised from four
     boxes to a cell test.
  3. Use T2's tube, with a D₀-restricted seed-lifting estimate, only for the thin R → 0 sliver at the
     family itself.
- The ~6·10⁵ survivors that do not touch D₀ need a finer or deeper layer prune, or the R-gap test.
  They are not a T2 problem.

## 5. Fixes and local work overnight

- **Phase-chart sliver in the rays path (bug fix, needed before certification).**
  - `fold_search` (complete.jl) built its starting phase boxes from Float64 `pi`. It therefore
    covered [−fl(π), fl(π)]⁵, which misses slivers of width about 1.2·10⁻¹⁶ at ±π.
  - Common rays with an entry −1 (φ_k = π) lie there, so the completeness argument could miss a
    ray.
  - The fix uses the chart [−p, p]⁵ with p = sup(interval(π)) ≥ π, shares split endpoints exactly,
    and makes the outer endpoints exactly ∓p. This is the convention cover.jl already used.
  - Regression: the g2 check cell (48 rays) and a pilot child (52 rays) give identical results.
  - The expansion never calls `fold_search`. The fix is synced before the certify sample.
- **Straggling tail and resumable expansion (efficiency fix during stage 1).**
  - Near the end, 141 slow tasks kept 28 nodes (3,584 cores) allocated. disBatch releases a
    node only when all its tasks finish.
  - `driver.jl expand` now resumes per job: on restart it keeps the records of jobs that have
    their closing `#prune` line and drops a partially written job. It was tested to give output
    identical to a clean run.
  - The tail was cancelled, and its 828 remaining level-6 jobs were split into per-job tasks
    (`split_tail.py`) on 7 nodes. The merge and audit require each job exactly once.
  - Results are unchanged, since expansion is deterministic.
  - **Cost of stage 1:** about 8.7k CPU-h for the cancelled nominal-cell run and about 25k CPU-h
    for the enlarged rerun. Together that is about 5.5% of the budget, about 2.5× the estimate.
  - The overrun comes from the BC and enlarged tests at every level, plus the idle tail.
  - **Lesson:** later stages use short tasks (8 cells for the certify sample) and `submit.sh …
    tail` to resubmit stragglers.
- **Review draft: an interval-verified rays certificate** (`Certification/review/verified_rays.jl`).
  It is not deployed.
  - Every float acceptance of the rays path is rechecked in outward interval arithmetic:
    - exclusion by the ClusterCover test on exactly re-expanded sub-cells;
    - regularity by an interval Jacobian;
    - centre exclusion by the interval test or an interval Krawczyk.
  - Ghost tubes are not used, so cells needing them fall to the cover certificate.
  - **Result on three reference cells** (g2 check cell, g2 neighbour, formerly-failed pilot child):
    same ray sets (48, 48, 52), no quartet, and **0 of the production path's float acceptances
    rejected** by the interval recheck (neither exclusion nor regularity).
  - Getting to 0 needed:
    - the interval chord test (unimodular entries);
    - the rigorous chord bound 2 sin(w/2) in the arc test;
    - an exact interval port of `regular_fast`: the Taylor-model preconditioner Y(s) and the
      closed-form dJ, with S₀, ρ, cos ranges and the norm in intervals.
  - Looser interval tests had rejected up to about 5·10⁵ acceptances per cell. That was
    looseness, not an error found.
  - Cost: 47–141 s against 5–31 s for production, 3–9× slower.
  - Suggested use: as an independent replay of a random sample of certified cells, or as a
    rigorous mode for the rays path. Deployment awaits review.
- **Cover-certificate speed (item c).** On a hard cell the outward-interval exclusion test
  dominates the cover search.
  - The rigorous chord test is cheaper per box: about 35 µs against 56 µs for the arc test.
  - On random boxes it decides every exclusion the arc test decides.
  - Trying chord before arc is a safe speed-up (review variant in `Certification/review/`).

## 7. Recommendation

**No-go for the full certification run as configured.** The unbiased 1% estimate is ≈ 1.8·10⁶ CPU-h
(3× the budget) with 24% of cells unresolved. Nearly all of the cost is failures, which run to the
40-minute cell cap. They concentrate at **R ≥ 250**; the R ≥ 300 band holds 60% of the work cells and 83% of the
cost.

**What to do next** (in order; each is cheap):
1. **Fix the cover certificate for wide ray clusters.**
   - Raise the label cap from 0.3 to 0.45 and add refinement levels 1/256 (and 1/512). Local tests
     rescue 5 of 6 failed depth-2 leaves and 3 of 6 failed depth-1 cells.
   - Make deep refinement node-efficient, refining only at label boundaries. That removes the search
     budget failures.
   - Retest on a few hundred of the sample's failed cells (≈ 10² CPU-h), then rerun an unbiased
     0.2% sample (≈ 10³ CPU-h) to re-project cost.
2. **Cost control.** A failed cell costs the whole 2,400 s cell cap.
   - Run the full campaign as a cheap first pass with a lower cell cap (e.g. 600–900 s): clean cells
     cost ~60 s.
   - Leave the hard high-R cells as a second-pass list with larger caps. The budget can then be
     spent where it is needed, and measured before it is spent.
3. **Not a T3 fix.** 60% of work cells have R ≥ 300. That band fails at 34% and costs 83% of the
   total. A neighbourhood of a two-parameter family such as X(α) cannot cover it. The lever is
   computational: the cover certificate at high R, where ray clusters are wide. An analytical
   agent could still help with a statement bounding the size of common-ray clusters at high R.
4. **Layer residual.**
   - About 6·10⁵ survivors that miss D₀: a deeper prune or the ResidualCells R-gap test.
   - About 1.6·10⁴ D₀-touching survivors (moderate R, median 39): bisect and certify.
   - T2's proved tube (1/16384) cannot absorb these by itself (section 4).
5. **Rigour.** The interval-verified rays path reproduces every float acceptance on the tested cells.
   Run it as an independent replay on a random sample of certified cells (`replay.jl`).
