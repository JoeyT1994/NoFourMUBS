# Radical-free mixed tests for two four-ray completions

**PROVED.** Suppose four retained rays on each side have already been
completed to two full six-ray reciprocal-orthogonal companions in the
rank-two-complement coordinates below. Their thirty-six mixed
unbiasedness equations are equivalent, as ideals in the stated localized
coefficient ring, to sixteen retained-ray tests, eight tests linear in
the squared completion parameters, and one bilinear test. The reduced
tests contain no square roots of the completion parameters.

**CERTIFIED (formal coefficient arithmetic).**
`verify_four_by_four_cross.py` verifies the general Laurent-polynomial
expansions and the row/column ideal identities with independent symbolic
inner products. It uses no six-dimensional quartet fixture and no
numerical computation.

**NOT ESTABLISHED HERE.** This note does not prove that a four-ray
completion exists, give a classification of its possible parameters,
or exclude any Fourier-free quartet. The completion parametrization and
its anchor equations must be supplied and checked separately.

## 1. Setup

Work over a commutative coefficient ring in which 6,c,d,u,v are units.
In particular 2,3 and16 are units. All transposes below are algebraic
transposes, and every reciprocal vector is a separate column; no
complex conjugation is imposed in this ring calculation.

The first companion consists of four retained reciprocal rays
(x_a,w_a), a=1,...,4, and the two completed columns

    x_sigma=(s+sigma p/u)/2,
    w_sigma=(t+sigma q u/c)/2,             sigma∈{+1,−1}.   (1)

The second consists of four retained rays (y_b,z_b), b=1,...,4, and

    y_tau=(S+tau r/v)/2,
    z_tau=(T+tau h v/d)/2,                 tau∈{+1,−1}.    (2)

Put U=u^2 and V=v^2. In a rank-two-complement chart, p,q are a selected
column and transposed row of the first residual rank-one matrix,
c is their common diagonal pivot, and r,h,d are the analogous data
on the second side. The proof below needs only (1)–(2) and the
completeness identities

    Σ_(a=1)^4 x_a w_a^T + Σ_sigma x_sigma w_sigma^T=6I,
    Σ_(b=1)^4 y_b z_b^T + Σ_tau   y_tau   z_tau^T=6I,      (3)

together with w_a^T x_a=w_sigma^T x_sigma=6 and the analogous norms
on the second side. These identities hold for a completed companion.

For any pair of columns define its mixed error by

    e(x,w;y,z)=(w^T y)(z^T x)−6.                          (4)

The full mixed ideal is generated by all36 errors in (4).

## 2. The sixteen retained tests

Retain

    (w_a^T y_b)(z_b^T x_a)−6=0             (a,b=1,...,4).   (5)

These sixteen tests are polynomial in the four-ray data and independent
of U,V.

## 3. The eight tests linear in U or V

For each retained first-side ray (x_a,w_a), retain

    A_a V+B_a=0,                                         (6)

where

    A_a=(w_a^T S)(h^T x_a),
    B_a=d(w_a^T r)(T^T x_a).

For each retained second-side ray (y_b,z_b), retain

    C_b U+D_b=0,                                         (7)

where

    C_b=(q^T y_b)(z_b^T s),
    D_b=c(t^T y_b)(z_b^T p).

Here the letters D_b denote scalar coefficients, not the residual
rank-one matrix used to obtain the coordinates.

### Derivation and ideal equivalence at this stage

Expansion of (2) gives the exact difference identity

    e(x_a,w_a;y_+,z_+)−e(x_a,w_a;y_−,z_−)
       =(A_a V+B_a)/(2dv).                               (8)

The analogous expansion of (1) gives

    e(x_+,w_+;y_b,z_b)−e(x_−,w_−;y_b,z_b)
       =(C_b U+D_b)/(2cu).                               (9)

By (3), the sum of all six errors in any mixed row or column is zero.
Modulo the sixteen retained errors (5), each pair of errors in (8)
has sum zero, and so does each pair in (9). Since2,c,d,u,v are units,
vanishing of the sixteen remaining errors involving one retained ray
is therefore equivalent to the eight scalar equations (6)–(7).

This is an equality of ideals modulo (5): for example, an omitted
error in (8) equals ±(A_aV+B_a)/(4dv), up to an explicit linear
combination of the four retained errors in its row. Conversely,
A_aV+B_a is2dv times the difference of the original two errors.

## 4. One bilinear test for the final two by two block

Define

    N(U,V) = a U V+b U+g V+e,                            (10)

where

    a=(q^T S)(h^T s),
    b=d(q^T r)(T^T s),
    g=c(t^T S)(h^T p),
    e=cd(t^T r)(T^T p).

The last retained condition is

    N(U,V)=0.                                            (11)

To verify it, expand the remaining mixed products in the four
characters1,sigma,tau,sigma*tau. Equations (1)–(2) give

    4 w_sigma^T y_tau
       =t^T S + tau(t^T r)/v + sigma u(q^T S)/c
                        +sigma*tau u(q^T r)/(cv),

    4 z_tau^T x_sigma
       =T^T s + sigma(T^T p)/u + tau v(h^T s)/d
                        +sigma*tau v(h^T p)/(du).         (12)

The sigma*tau coefficient of their product divided by16 is

    delta = (1/16)[ (t^T S)(h^T p)v/(du)
                    +(t^T r)(T^T p)/(uv)
                    +(q^T S)(h^T s)uv/(cd)
                    +(q^T r)(T^T s)u/(cv) ]
          = N(U,V)/(16 c d u v).                         (13)

Modulo the24 equations already retained, the final two by two error
matrix has zero row sums and zero column sums, by (3). Every such
matrix is

              [ delta  −delta ]
              [               ].
              [−delta   delta ]

Equivalently e_sigma,tau=sigma*tau*delta. Thus all four errors vanish
exactly when (11) holds.

This again proves ideal equality: modulo the24 earlier generators,
each final error is a unit multiple of N(U,V), with sign. In the
other direction, N(U,V) is4cduv times the signed sum of the four
original final errors. There is no passage to radicals and no
assumption about simple roots or nonsingular solutions.

## 5. Scope of the radical-free reduction

The reduced mixed system comprises

    16 retained tests + 4 equations in V + 4 equations in U
                      +1 equation bilinear in U,V.       (14)

Every coefficient in (14) is obtained by matrix arithmetic from the
two retained four-ray systems and their selected pivots. The included
`FOUR_RAY_COMPLETION.md` determines U and V by rational expressions on
its physical coefficient charts. Those expressions may be substituted in (6), (7), and
(11), with their nonzero denominators recorded. No sign choice for u
or v is needed: changing either sign only exchanges its completed
columns and leaves (14) unchanged.

Over a base in which U,V are units, adjoining roots u^2=U and v^2=V
is a finite free extension of rank four. Because2 is invertible it is
also étale. Consequently the ideal-equivalence calculation may be
checked after adjoining those roots without losing any equation on
the base. Physical feasibility still requires the separate physical
conditions on the retained rays and on their completions; a complex
root of this algebraic system is not automatically a physical
quartet.

This gives a concrete smaller input for a direct F′ elimination. It
does not by itself produce such an elimination or an atlas-free
exclusion.

## 6. Physical coefficient symmetry and an exact residual

When the retained rays and their completions are physical, the
rank-two-complement construction gives

    t=conjugate(s), q=conjugate(p),
    T=conjugate(S), h=conjugate(r),
    c,d>0,          |u|^2=c, |v|^2=d.

The coefficients in (10) therefore obey

    e=cd conjugate(a),       g=(c/d) conjugate(b).         (15)

Consequently

    N(U,V)/(uv)=2 Re(a u v+b u/v),                        (16)

and delta in (13) is real. Thus the final two by two obstruction is
one real scalar, although writing N=0 in unrestricted complex
coordinates produces two real equations.

If the other24 reduced mixed tests hold, all mixed errors except the
last four vanish. The exact raw squared error is

    Σ_(all a,b) [(w_a^T y_b)(z_b^T x_a)−6]^2
       =4 delta^2=|N(U,V)|^2/(64 c^3 d^3).                (17)

For normalized bases, whose overlap probabilities are q_ab/36, the
squared error from the target probability1/6 is (17) divided by1296.
The coefficient verifier checks (15)–(17) as formal Laurent identities
under the displayed conjugation rules. They are exact residual formulas,
not lower bounds or global exclusions.
