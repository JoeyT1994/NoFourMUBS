## 2. Complete ray partition

A unit vector unbiased to the standard basis, modulo its overall phase, can be
written

\[
v(\beta)=\frac1{\sqrt6}(1,e^{i\beta_1},\ldots,e^{i\beta_5}),
\qquad \beta_j\in[0,2\pi].
\]

Start with all \(12^5\) closed boxes. At resolution \(M\), a bin has midpoint

\[
u_0=0,\qquad u_j=(2b_j+1)\pi/M,
\qquad b_j\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}.
\]

The displacement satisfies \(x_0=0\) and \(|x_j|\le a=\pi/M\). Its mean-free
representative has bounds

\[
X=\sqrt{29/6}\,a,\qquad \|x\|_1\le5a,\qquad
\|x\|_\infty\le3a/2.
\]

These follow by convexity from the 32 cube vertices. For example, the squared
centered norm at a vertex is

\[
5a^2-\frac16\left(\sum_{j=1}^5x_j\right)^2\le\frac{29}6a^2,
\]

because the sum of five signs has absolute value at least one. Common phases
do not affect any modulus below, so the centered representative may be used in
norm bounds while the original box is used in coordinate support bounds.

At every visited node the replay proves one of three statements: the entire
closed bin is inside a declared label ball; a necessary unbiasedness inequality
is violated throughout the closed bin; or all 32 closed children are visited.
It reconstructs the child indices itself. A stored list of purported surviving
bins is never accepted as a cover. Final success requires zero unfinished
leaves.

## 3. Scalar residual and linear support

For a bin midpoint define

\[
q_{ji}=6^{-1/2}\exp\bigl(i(u_j-2\pi ji/6)\bigr),\quad
S_i=\sum_jq_{ji},\quad c_i=(|S_i|^2-1)/2,
\]

\[
f_i(t)=\frac12\left(\left|\sum_jq_{ji}e^{it_j}\right|^2-1\right),
\qquad J_{ij}=-\operatorname{Im}(\overline S_iq_{ji}).
\]

Unbiasedness to \(H/\sqrt6\) means

\[
f_i(x-\delta_{:,i})=0\quad(0\le i<6).
\]

For any real witness \(y\in\mathbb R^6\), put \(C_{ji}=y_iJ_{ij}\). Each
column of \(C\) sums to zero. Its horizontal and normal coefficient norms are

\[
h^2=\|P_{\mathrm{hor}}C\|_F^2
=\sum_{ij}y_i^2J_{ij}^2-\frac16\|J^Ty\|^2,
\quad b=\|P_TC\|_F,
\quad c_N=\sqrt{h^2-b^2}.
\]

The ordinary physical linear support is bounded by

\[
\Lambda(C)=
\begin{cases}
\rho h,&nh\ge\rho c_N,\\
\sqrt{\rho^2-n^2}\,b+nc_N,&nh<\rho c_N.
\end{cases}
\]

This maximizes \(b\sqrt{\rho^2-t^2}+c_Nt\) over \(0\le t\le n\). An uncertain
interval branch comparison uses the always valid \(\rho h\) bound. The replay
also permits the always valid \(\rho b+nc_N\) bound. The bin support is

\[
L_x=a\sum_{j>0}|(J^Ty)_j|.
\]

Direct component and vector tests use respectively

\[
\bigl||S_i|-1\bigr|\le5a/\sqrt6+r,
\qquad \bigl\||S|-\mathbf1\bigr\|_2\le X+\rho.
\]

The latter follows from the Fourier operator norm and the Frobenius phase
bound. Scalar Hessian tests use

\[
|c_i|\le a\sum_{j>0}|J_{ij}|+\Lambda(C_i)
+\frac12(1+|S_i|/\sqrt6)(X+r)^2.
\]

The additional first-order weighted test, certificate code 14, uses
\(s_i=y_iS_i\), \(g=\max_j|\sum_i\overline{s_i}q_{ji}|\), and the remainder

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_2={1\over2}\bigl[&
\min\{\|y\|_\infty(X+\rho)^2,\|y\|_1(X+r)^2\}\\
&+\min\{gX^2,\|s\|_2(3a/2)X\}\\
&+{2X\over\sqrt6}\min\{\|s\|_2\rho,\|s\|_1r\}\\
&+{1\over\sqrt6}\min\{\|s\|_\infty\rho^2,\|s\|_1r^2\}\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\]

Its rejection condition is \(|y\cdot c|>L_x+\Lambda(C)+E_2\). These bounds
follow by expanding \(e^{i(x_j-\delta_{ji})}\), using
\(|e^{it}-1|\le|t|\) and \(|e^{it}-1-it|\le t^2/2\), and summing the
shared-bin term before taking absolute values. All witnesses are rational with
denominator \(2^{20}\).

## 4. Collected Hessian and its projections

The exact midpoint Hessian is

\[
(H_i)_{jk}=\operatorname{Re}(q_{ji}\overline{q_{ki}})
-\mathbf1_{j=k}\operatorname{Re}(\overline S_iq_{ji}),
\qquad H_i\mathbf1=0.
\]

Let \(\mathcal H=\operatorname{diag}(y_iH_i)\), with physical column blocks,
and let \(T_i\) be the restriction of the tangent frame to column \(i\). Define

\[
H_{xx}=\sum_i y_iH_i,\quad
H_{xT}=\sum_i y_iH_iT_i,\quad
H_{TT}=\sum_iT_i^Ty_iH_iT_i.
\]

The exact squared projection norms are

\[
h_{TN}^2=\|P_N\mathcal HT\|_F^2
=\sum_i\|y_iH_iT_i\|_F^2-\frac16\|H_{xT}\|_F^2-\|H_{TT}\|_F^2,
\]

\[
h_{xN,j}^2=\|P_N\mathcal HE_j\|^2
=\sum_i\|y_iH_ie_j\|^2-\frac16\|H_{xx}e_j\|^2
-\|(H_{xT})_{j,:}\|^2.
\]

Here \(E_j\) copies the shared ray coordinate into all physical columns. These
identities use the original (H_{TT}), before any predictor is collected into
it. Directed interval evaluation is retained through every subtraction; only
the lower endpoint of a known squared norm may be clipped at zero.

Write \(Q(\eta,\eta)=\sum_{ab}t_at_bQ_{ab}\), and define

\[
(M_Q)_{ab}=\langle C,Q_{ab}\rangle,
\qquad A=H_{TT}-2M_Q.
\]

This combines the quadratic term \(\tfrac12\eta^T\mathcal H\eta\) with the
linear physical term \(-\langle C,Q(\eta,\eta)\rangle\), with their signs
preserved. Symmetric operator norms are bounded by the smaller of Frobenius
norm and maximum absolute row sum. Let \(h_H\) be the corresponding bound for
the block-diagonal matrix \(\mathcal H\).

For code 17 the remaining quadratic bound is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Q_3={}&{a^2\over2}\sum_{j,k>0}|(H_{xx})_{jk}|
+a\rho\sum_{j>0}\|(H_{xT})_{j,:}\|
+{\rho^2\over2}\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\\
&+\epsilon_Q\rho\,h_{TN}
+an\sum_{j>0}h_{xN,j}+{n^2\over2}h_H.
\end{aligned}
\]

The normal-tangent term uses \(\epsilon_Q\), because its leading cubic term
\(\eta^T\mathcal H Q(\eta,\eta)\) is retained explicitly in the next
section. Terms involving the full normal displacement still use \(n\).

## 5. Exact pure tangent cubic

The cubic term after collecting both normal predictors is

\[
\mathcal C(t,t,t)
=\eta^T\mathcal H Q(\eta,\eta)-\langle C,P_3(\eta)\rangle
-{1\over6}\sum_i y_iD^3f_i(0)[\eta_{:,i}]^3.
\]

The final sign comes from evaluating the pure physical displacement at
\(-\eta\). Direct differentiation gives

\[
D^3f_i(0)[z]^3
=\sum_{j<k}\operatorname{Im}(q_{ji}\overline{q_{ki}})(z_j-z_k)^3.
\]

The exact replay constructs the 20 symmetric coefficients of \(\mathcal C\).
It converts the rational natural coefficients of \(P_3\) to symmetric
trilinear coefficients by dividing each polynomial coefficient by the number
of distinct permutations of its indices, then applies the explicit tangent
frame above. It checks the natural quadratic polynomial in the two tensor
files agrees exactly, including the factor of two on mixed monomials.

Let (C_{a,(b,c)}) be the \(4\times16\) flattening of this symmetric cubic
tensor. For \(\|t\|\le\rho\),

\[
|\mathcal C(t,t,t)|\le\rho^3\kappa,
\qquad
\kappa=\min\left\{\|\mathcal C\|_F,
\sqrt{\max_a\sum_b|(CC^T)_{ab}|}\right\}.
\]

The Frobenius norm counts each distinct coefficient with its permutation
multiplicity. The second bound is the Gram-matrix row-sum bound on the
flattening's operator norm. Both are upper bounds; no numerical eigenvalue is
trusted.

## 6. Sharp global mixed-cubic and quartic remainders

For every centered six-vector \(z\),

\[
\sum_{j<k}(z_j-z_k)^2=6\|z\|^2,
\quad \sum_jz_j^4\le{7\over10}\|z\|^4,
\quad \sum_{j<k}(z_j-z_k)^4\le{36\over5}\|z\|^4.
\]

Here is an exact proof of the improved fourth moment. Put
\(P=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^T/6\) and \(B(v)=P\operatorname{diag}(v)P\).
For centered \(v\), \(\|B(v)\|_F^2=(2/3)\|v\|^2\) and \(B(v)\perp P\).
The squared residual after projecting \(zz^T\) onto these two orthogonal spaces
is

\[
{21\over20}\|z\|^4-{3\over2}\sum_jz_j^4\ge0.
\]

The replay verifies the rational Gram identities for \(P\) and all six
coordinate matrices (B\(e_j\)). Expanding the pair differences then proves the
displayed \(36/5\) bound. Cauchy--Schwarz and Hölder consequently give, for any
three centered vectors,

\[
{1\over36}\sum_{j<k}|(z_j-z_k)(w_j-w_k)(v_j-v_k)|
\le{\|z\|\|w\|\|v\|\over\sqrt{30}}.
\]

This controls the third derivative globally, since every pair coefficient has
modulus \(1/6\). Put

\[
\alpha=\rho/\sqrt3,\qquad \beta=\sqrt{5/6}\,n,
\quad y_1=\|y\|_1,\ y_2=\|y\|_2,\ y_\infty=\|y\|_\infty.
\]

Define the weighted normal-containing cubic bounds

\[
s_1=\min\{y_\infty\alpha\rho n,\ y_2\alpha^2n,\ y_1\alpha^2\beta\},
\]

\[
s_2=\min\{y_\infty\alpha n^2,\ y_2\beta^2\rho,\ y_1\alpha\beta^2\},
\qquad
s_3=\min\{y_\infty\beta n^2,\ y_2\beta^2n,\ y_1\beta^3\}.
\]

The third-order terms containing at least one normal factor are at most

\[
R_{3,N}=(3s_1+3s_2+s_3)/\sqrt{30}.
\]

The change in the remainder of order at least three caused by the common bin
displacement is at most

\[
R_{3,x}={1\over\sqrt{30}}\left[
y_1X^3+3X^2\min\{y_2\rho,y_1r\}
+3X\min\{y_\infty\rho^2,y_1r^2\}\right].
\]

This is a global Taylor integral bound for the change between \(f(-\delta)\)
and \(f(x-\delta)\) after subtracting their common quadratic expansion. Thus
the separate quartic remainder below only needs the pure physical increment;
there is no omitted quartic bin term.

Expanding the pure physical increment to degree three gives

\[
R_{4,\delta}\le{1\over144}\sum_i|y_i|
\sum_{j<k}(\delta_{ji}-\delta_{ki})^4
\le {1\over20}\min\{y_\infty r^2\rho^2,y_1r^4\}.
\]

Every weighted minimum follows from either the total Frobenius norm, the
individual column norm, or Cauchy--Schwarz. In particular, the proof never
assumes that six physical columns can each attain the total radius
independently.

## 7. The code-17 rejection

All feasible rays in a bin satisfy

\[
\boxed{
|y\cdot c|\le L_x+\rho b+\epsilon_3c_N
+\mathcal Q_3+\rho^3\kappa+R_{3,N}+R_{3,x}+R_{4,\delta}.}
\]

A strict reverse inequality rejects the entire closed bin. The leading
quadratic and cubic terms have been collected algebraically before any norm
bound is applied. The only stored witness is \(y\in2^{-20}\mathbb Z^6\),
normalized to \(\|y\|_\infty\le1\). Every coefficient, projection, norm bound,
and remainder is reconstructed during replay.

Code 16 is the cheaper quadratic-predictor test. It replaces the linear
physical support by \(\rho b+\epsilon_Qc_N\), retains \(A=H_{TT}-2M_Q\), uses

\(n\rho h_{TN}\) in the quadratic bound, and bounds the entire cubic remainder
by

\[
\min\left\{\frac{2a+d}{6},\frac{\sqrt3}{9}(X+r)\right\}
\left[y_1X^2+2X\min\{y_2\rho,y_1r\}
+\min\{y_\infty\rho^2,y_1r^2\}\right],
\]

where

\[
d=\min\left\{\sqrt2r,
\sqrt{7/18}\sqrt{\rho^2-n^2}+\sqrt{23/18}\,n\right\}.
\]

The second row-difference bound follows by horizontal/tangent/normal
projection of the coordinate functional. Its full squared norm is \(5/3\);
the tangent squared norm is at most \(7/18\), checked for every row pair and
column with rational arithmetic. The required cap condition
\(n^2/\rho^2\le23/30\) is checked explicitly.

## 8. Signed tangent support: certificate code 18

The previous code-17 inequality bounds the tangent linear and quadratic terms
separately. The additional code-18 inequality retains their shared tangent
argument and their signs. Define the four-vector

\[
b_a=\langle C,T_a\rangle,
\qquad A=H_{TT}-2M_Q.
\]

The residual equation implies

\[
y\cdot c
\le \sup_{\|t\|\le\rho}\left(b^Tt-\tfrac12t^TAt\right)
+L_x+\epsilon_3c_N+\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{rest}}
+\rho^3\kappa+R_{3,N}+R_{3,x}+R_{4,\delta},
\]

where \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{rest}}\) is the code-17 quadratic bound with its
\(\rho^2\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}/2\) term removed. The separate linear tangent
bound \(\rho\|b\|\) is also absent. All remaining terms are unchanged.

For any rational \(\lambda\ge0\) such that

\[
P=A+2\lambda I\succ0,
\]

completion of the square proves

\[
\begin{aligned}
b^Tt-\tfrac12t^TAt
&=\lambda\|t\|^2+b^Tt-\tfrac12t^TPt\\
&\le\lambda\rho^2+\tfrac12b^TP^{-1}b.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus a valid finite witness is the seven integers representing
\((y_0,\ldots,y_5,\lambda)\) at denominator \(2^{20}\). The numerical search
may propose \(\lambda\) using floating-point eigendecomposition and a secular
search. The exact replay never uses that numerical eigendecomposition. It
forms \(P\) itself and computes a directed-interval factorization
\(P=LDL^T\). Every diagonal pivot must have a strictly positive lower endpoint.
This proves positive definiteness. With \(w=L^{-1}b\), the replay encloses

\[
b^TP^{-1}b=\sum_{k=0}^3\frac{w_k^2}{D_k}
\]

by outward interval arithmetic. There is no unverified inverse or condition
number estimate. If a pivot cannot be proved positive, that witness fails.

Code 18 compares the **signed** lower endpoint of \(y\cdot c\) to the upper
endpoint of the complete right-hand side. It does not substitute
\(|y\cdot c|\) into this one-sided inequality. The numerical proposal orients
\(y\) to make its scalar residual positive, but the replay checks the signed
inequality directly. This protects the sign of the tangent quadratic term.

Codes 16 and 17 are attempted first during discovery. The more expensive LDL
certificate is stored only when the signed support improvement is needed.
